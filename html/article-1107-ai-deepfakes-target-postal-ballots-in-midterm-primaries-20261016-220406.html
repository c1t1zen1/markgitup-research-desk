AI Deepfakes Target Postal Ballots in Midterm Primaries

AI-generated voice calls are emerging as a targeted tool to disrupt mail-in ballot participation in primary elections, prompting new legal scrutiny and state-level regulatory responses ahead of the 2026 midterms.

Why this matters now

This article examines the specific use of AI deepfake voice technology to target voters who use postal ballots during primary elections. The evidence base consists of federal agency alerts, legal analyses, and election security tracking reports from 2024-2026. While direct evidence of postal-ballot-specific deepfake campaigns is still emerging, the New Hampshire robocall incident and subsequent legal rulings establish a clear precedent for this tactic's potential to suppress turnout among mail-in voters.

Assignment angle: Focuses on the specific, emerging tactic of AI-generated voice calls targeting voters who use mail-in ballots during primary elections, a distinct vector from general deepfake video or social media misinformation.

FIELD NOTE

The New Hampshire Precedent

The first documented case of AI deepfake voice calls targeting voters occurred in New Hampshire's 2024 presidential primary. Voters received calls impersonating President Joe Biden, urging them not to vote by mail. The calls were part of a coordinated effort to suppress turnout among Democratic voters who typically use postal ballots.

This incident established the basic playbook: use AI voice cloning to impersonate a trusted figure, deliver a simple message discouraging mail-in voting, and target specific demographic groups. The calls were sophisticated enough to fool many recipients, demonstrating the technology's potential for electoral manipulation.

  • First documented AI deepfake robocall targeting voters
  • Impersonated President Biden to discourage mail-in voting
  • Targeted New Hampshire primary voters in 2024
  • Established template for future campaigns

Legal Response and Regulatory Framework

Following the New Hampshire incident, the Federal Election Commission ruled in February 2024 that AI-generated voice clones count as 'artificial or prerecorded voices' under the Telephone Consumer Protection Act. This made deepfake robocalls illegal without prior consent, providing a legal basis for prosecuting similar campaigns.

However, enforcement remains challenging. The ruling applies to calls made without consent, but determining consent in political contexts is complex. Additionally, many deepfake calls originate from outside the United States or use spoofed caller IDs, making tracing and prosecution difficult. Criminal charges were filed in the New Hampshire case, but the legal process is ongoing.

  • FEC ruled AI voice clones fall under TCPA in February 2024
  • Deepfake robocalls now illegal without prior consent
  • Criminal charges filed in New Hampshire case
  • Enforcement challenges remain with spoofed calls

Federal Agency Warnings

The FBI and CISA have issued joint alerts warning election officials about AI deepfake threats, including phishing, smishing, and deepfake content targeting election teams. While these alerts focus primarily on election infrastructure rather than voter-targeted calls, they acknowledge the growing sophistication of AI-generated content in the electoral landscape.

The agencies recommend phishing-resistant multi-factor authentication and out-of-band verification checks for election officials. No breach of election systems has been reported, but the warnings suggest that voter-targeted deepfake campaigns are part of a broader ecosystem of AI-driven electoral manipulation.

  • FBI and CISA issued joint alert on AI deepfake threats
  • Warnings include phishing, smishing, and deepfake content
  • No election system breaches reported
  • Recommendations focus on authentication and verification

State-Level Responses

As of 2026, more than 20 states have enacted or proposed legislation addressing AI deepfakes in elections. These laws vary significantly in their approach, with some requiring disclosure of AI-generated content, others imposing time-based bans on deepfake ads near election day, and still others creating specific penalties for deepfake robocalls.

The regulatory landscape is fragmented, making it difficult for national campaigns to comply uniformly. Some states have adopted stricter standards than federal law, while others lag behind. This patchwork approach may allow deepfake campaigns to exploit jurisdictional gaps, particularly in states with weaker regulations or enforcement mechanisms.

  • Approaches vary: disclosure requirements, time-based bans, penalties
  • Fragmented regulatory landscape complicates compliance

Closing read

Conclusion

The evidence indicates that AI deepfake voice calls targeting mail-in ballot voters represent a real and growing threat to election integrity, particularly in primary elections where turnout is lower and targeted messaging is more effective. The New Hampshire incident demonstrated the tactic's potential, and subsequent legal and regulatory responses suggest that policymakers recognize the threat.

However, the evidence also shows significant gaps. Direct documentation of postal-ballot-specific deepfake campaigns beyond New Hampshire is limited. Most federal and state responses focus on broader AI deepfake threats rather than the specific vector of mail-in ballot suppression. The legal framework is evolving but remains incomplete, with enforcement challenges that may allow the tactic to persist.

Projections suggest that as AI voice technology becomes more sophisticated and accessible, we can expect more frequent and subtle deepfake campaigns targeting specific voter demographics. The key question is whether regulatory and legal responses will keep pace with technological advancement.

Five takeaways

  • AI deepfake voice calls have already been used to target mail-in ballot voters, as demonstrated in New Hampshire's 2024 primary
  • The FEC has ruled that AI-generated voice clones fall under existing robocall regulations, providing a legal basis for prosecution
  • Federal agencies warn of growing AI deepfake threats to elections, though no system breaches have been reported
  • More than 20 states are addressing AI deepfakes in elections, but the regulatory landscape remains fragmented
  • The tactic of targeting mail-in ballot voters with deepfake calls is likely to grow as AI voice technology improves and becomes more accessible
