AI-Generated Policy Briefs Now Shape Congressional Staff Research

Behind-the-scenes use of generative AI by congressional staff is emerging as a new layer of influence in US elections, reshaping how policy analysis is drafted and potentially affecting legislative priorities.

Why this matters now

This article examines the growing role of AI in shaping policy briefs and research for congressional staff, a development that extends AI's influence in US elections beyond voter-facing tools. The evidence base includes a Stanford HAI congressional boot camp on AI, RAND's AI resources for policymakers, a CRS report on AI-focused products, a White House policy framework, a Washington Post AI & Tech Brief, and an NCSL survey of legislative staff. AI in Congressional Staff Workflows

Congressional staff play a key role in shaping and developing policy on critical technology areas such as artificial intelligence, according to a Stanford HAI congressional boot camp on AI. The rapid advancements in AI have prompted efforts to educate staff on its applications and implications. RAND's AI resources for congressional policymakers include research, publications, testimonies, and briefs relevant to public policy and legislative activity, suggesting a growing infrastructure to support AI-informed decision-making. Policy Implications and Recommendations

The framework's focus on supporting wider deployment of AI tools across American industry indicates a broader strategic vision for AI's role in the economy and society.
