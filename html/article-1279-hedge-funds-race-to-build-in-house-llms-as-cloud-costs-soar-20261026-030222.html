Hedge Funds Race to Build In-House LLMs as Cloud Costs Soar

Hedge funds are shifting from cloud API dependence to in-house LLM development, driven by cost pressures, latency needs, and the strategic value of proprietary models.

October 26, 2026 · 3:02 AM PDT · 5 min read · 12 sources · Original family: LLMs in Hedge Funds & Trading

Editor’s brief

Why this matters now

This article examines the growing trend of hedge funds building internal AI labs and developing proprietary large language models, rather than relying on third-party cloud APIs. Evidence was gathered from industry reporting on fund strategies, including coverage of firms like Millennium and Morteon, as well as analysis of the strategic trade-offs between building in-house versus buying AI capabilities. The sources consistently point to a strategic dilemma facing funds: whether to partner with vendors or develop their own research models. This shift appears driven by multiple factors including cost considerations, the desire for competitive differentiation, and the need for low-latency trading systems.

Assignment angle: Shift from cloud API dependence to proprietary model development driven by cost and latency pressures.

FIELD NOTE

The Build vs. Buy Dilemma

Hedge funds face a fundamental strategic choice regarding their AI capabilities: partner with third-party vendors or develop research models in-house. This decision is becoming increasingly critical as AI becomes central to trading strategies and competitive advantage. The choice affects not just immediate costs but long-term strategic positioning in the market.

Some of the most advanced funds have largely moved away from purchasing end-to-end AI platforms. Instead, small internal teams are codifying the fund's own research frameworks into AI workflows while purchasing the underlying data. This approach suggests a preference for customization and control over off-the-shelf solutions.

The trend toward in-house development reflects a broader recognition that proprietary AI capabilities can provide a sustainable competitive edge that purchased solutions cannot match.

  • Funds must choose between third-party vendors and in-house model development
  • Advanced funds increasingly favor building over buying end-to-end platforms
  • In-house teams focus on codifying proprietary research frameworks into AI workflows

Leading Funds Invest in AI Labs

Major hedge funds including Millennium and Morteon are actively hiring researchers and establishing in-house AI labs. These investments aim to develop proprietary models, data infrastructure, and trading systems that provide a competitive edge in the market.

The establishment of dedicated AI labs represents a significant commitment of resources and talent. Rather than treating AI as a tool to be purchased, these funds are approaching it as a core competency to be developed internally.

This investment pattern suggests that leading funds view proprietary AI capabilities as essential to maintaining or gaining market advantage, rather than as a supplementary technology.

  • Millennium and Morteon among funds building in-house AI labs
  • Focus on developing proprietary models, data, and trading systems
  • Significant hiring of AI researchers indicates long-term commitment

FIELD NOTE

The DeepSeek Precedent

The emergence of DeepSeek from hedge fund High-Flyer, led by Liang Wengfeng, demonstrates the close relationship between quantitative investment and AI model development. This example illustrates how the skills and infrastructure developed for trading can translate into world-class AI research.

The DeepSeek case suggests that hedge funds may be uniquely positioned to develop advanced AI models, given their existing investments in data infrastructure, computational resources, and quantitative talent.

This precedent may encourage other funds to view in-house AI development not just as a cost consideration but as a potential source of innovation and even new business opportunities.

  • DeepSeek spun out from High-Flyer hedge fund
  • Demonstrates intertwinement between quantitative investment and AI development
  • Suggests funds have unique advantages in AI model development

AI's Role in Hedge Fund Research

Hedge funds are increasingly using AI across their research operations, from alternative data ingestion to equity research automation and portfolio monitoring. This broad application creates additional incentive to develop in-house capabilities that can be tailored to specific research needs.

The integration of AI into core research workflows suggests that off-the-shelf solutions may not adequately address the specialized requirements of hedge fund research. Custom-built systems can be more closely aligned with a fund's specific strategies and data sources.

As AI becomes more embedded in research processes, the value of proprietary AI capabilities grows, further incentivizing in-house development.

  • AI used for alt-data ingestion, equity research automation, and portfolio monitoring
  • Integration into core workflows increases value of custom solutions
  • Specialized research needs may not be met by generic platforms

Closing read

Conclusion

The evidence suggests a clear trend toward hedge funds building in-house AI capabilities rather than relying solely on third-party cloud APIs. Multiple sources confirm that leading funds are investing in dedicated AI labs and proprietary model development, driven by the strategic value of differentiation and potentially by cost and latency considerations.

However, the sources do not provide specific data on cloud API costs or latency benchmarks that would definitively explain the shift. The decision appears multifaceted, involving strategic positioning, competitive advantage, and operational efficiency. The DeepSeek example from High-Flyer hedge fund demonstrates that this approach can yield world-class AI research, potentially validating the investment for other funds.

While the trend is evident among leading funds, it remains unclear whether smaller funds can or will follow suit given the significant resources required for in-house AI development. The build vs. buy decision may ultimately depend on fund size, strategy, and competitive position.

Five takeaways

  • Hedge funds face a strategic choice between buying AI platforms and building in-house capabilities
  • Leading funds like Millennium and Morteon are investing in proprietary AI labs and model development
  • The DeepSeek emergence from High-Flyer hedge fund demonstrates the potential of in-house AI research
  • Advanced funds increasingly favor building custom AI workflows over purchasing end-to-end platforms
  • AI integration across research operations increases the value of proprietary, tailored solutions
