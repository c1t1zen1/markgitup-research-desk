AI Firms' Lobbying Surge Ahead of 2026 Election: Pre-emption Clauses Target State AI Laws

Tech firms are pushing federal preemption to override state AI laws, but states enacted 109 AI laws while Congress stalled.

October 9, 2026 · 5:18 AM PDT · 3 min read · 12 sources · Original family: AI Lobbying & Policy in Washington
AI policylobbyingfederalismregulation
Editor’s brief

Why this matters now

The evidence reveals a multi-front preemption fight unfolding across federal and state lines. Sources show the Trump administration issued executive orders directing DOJ to challenge state AI laws, while Congress considered—but ultimately stripped—a 10-year preemption provision. Meanwhile, states enacted over 100 AI laws, creating a regulatory patchwork that compliance teams must navigate.

The sources conflict on scope: one source claims 29 states passed AI rules, while another cites 109 AI laws enacted. The core tension is that federal preemption efforts have repeatedly failed, leaving companies to navigate competing state requirements. The 2026 midterms loom as a political deadline, though sources do not explicitly connect lobbying to that election.

Assignment angle: Tech companies are deploying a new lobbying strategy to push federal preemption clauses that would override the patchwork of state-level AI regulations, framing it as a competitiveness issue ahead of the 2026 midterms.

FIELD NOTE

The Federal Preemption Push

The White House published a National Policy Framework for AI in March 2026, pushing Congress to preempt state AI laws. However, the Senate voted 99-1 to strip a 10-year state-preemption provision from a major bill in mid-2026, dealing a significant setback to preemption advocates.

Separately, the executive branch acted unilaterally. In December 2025, President Trump signed an executive order directing DOJ to challenge state AI laws on preemption and Commerce Clause grounds. By April 2026, the United States intervened in X. AI LLC v. Weiser, joining xAI's challenge to Colorado's AI Act.

  • DOJ task force challenging state laws on Commerce Clause grounds
  • US intervened in xAI's challenge to Colorado AI Act

FIELD NOTE

State Regulatory Patchwork

States have not waited for federal action. Sources indicate states enacted 109 AI laws and 28 data-center laws, with 1,561 AI bills introduced across state legislatures in 2026 alone. One source frames this as 29 states passing their own rules with no comprehensive federal law.

Colorado exemplifies state-level dynamism. Its legislature passed SB 26-189 on May 14, 2026, which repealed and reenacted the Colorado AI Act—amending rather than abandoning its AI regulatory framework.

What Comes Next

Federal court challenges to state AI laws on Commerce Clause grounds are expected by late 2026, with potential implications for all state AI regulation. The DOJ's intervention in the Colorado case could establish judicial precedent.

Congressional action on the SAFE Innovation Framework Act or similar legislation could advance in 2027, with preemption provisions as the central battleground. However, the 99-1 Senate vote suggests limited congressional appetite for broad preemption.

Closing read

Conclusion

The evidence shows a clear pattern: federal preemption efforts have repeatedly stalled while state legislative activity accelerates. The executive branch's DOJ challenge to state laws represents an alternative pathway, but its outcome remains uncertain.

Sources conflict on exact figures—109 AI laws versus 29 states with rules—suggesting the patchwork is growing faster than tracking mechanisms can reconcile. The 2026 midterms are not explicitly linked to lobbying in the provided sources, so any connection remains speculative.

What is clear is that companies face a compliance landscape that grows more complex as states continue legislating independently.
