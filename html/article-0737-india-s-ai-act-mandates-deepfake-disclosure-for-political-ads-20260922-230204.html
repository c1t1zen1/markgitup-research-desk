India's AI Act Mandates Deepfake Disclosure for Political Ads

India's 2026 IT Rules require political deepfakes to be labeled and removed within hours, marking a major shift in synthetic media regulation for elections.

Editor’s brief

Why this matters now

India has enacted comprehensive new rules governing synthetic media, including deepfakes, with specific implications for political advertising. The IT Rules Amendment 2026, effective February 20, 2026, mandates disclosure of AI-generated content and imposes strict takedown timelines. This represents a significant regulatory approach distinct from U.S. and EU frameworks, focusing on intermediary accountability and rapid response mechanisms. The evidence gathered from legal analyses, government advisories, and industry commentary indicates a robust but still-evolving regulatory landscape.

Assignment angle: This focuses on India's new statutory requirements for deepfake disclosure in political advertising, a distinct jurisdictional and regulatory angle not covered by previous U.S. or EU-focused pieces.

FIELD NOTE

Disclosure Requirements for Political Deepfakes

Under the new IT Rules 2026, all synthetically generated information (SGI), including deepfakes and AI-cloned audio, must carry clear disclaimers and permanent provenance metadata. The rules specify that non-prohibited SGI must be 'clearly and prominently labelled,' with visual SGI requiring visual labels and audio SGI requiring audio disclosures. This applies to political advertising, meaning candidates and parties using AI-generated content must disclose its synthetic nature to voters. The requirement shifts the burden to content creators and platforms rather than relying solely on viewer detection.

  • All SGI must carry disclaimers and provenance metadata effective February 20, 2026
  • Visual deepfakes require visual labels; audio deepfakes require audio disclosures
  • Political ads using synthetic media must disclose AI generation to voters
  • Labels must be 'clear and prominent' rather than fixed-size technical specifications

FIELD NOTE

Rapid Takedown Mechanisms

The amendment introduces strict timelines for removing deceptive deepfakes. Platforms must takedown deepfakes within 3 hours of notification, a significant acceleration from previous standards. This rapid response requirement applies particularly to 'Prohibited SGI,' which includes deceptive political or electoral content, impersonation or fraud-oriented deepfakes, and forged documents capable of misleading the public. Intermediaries must also warn users against generating or disseminating these categories and ensure their product design does not actively nudge users toward harmful deepfakes.

  • 3-hour takedown deadline for notified deepfakes
  • Applies to deceptive political content, impersonation, and fraud-oriented deepfakes
  • Platforms must warn users about prohibited SGI categories
  • Product design must not nudge users toward harmful deepfakes

Political and Electoral Implications

The rules have direct implications for electoral integrity. Political candidates using deepfakes to maliciously slander opponents risk disqualification under the Representation of People Act. The Election Commission's recent advisories align with the regulatory direction, suggesting transparency, traceability, and rapid correction as the most workable approach. This disclosure-based model is viewed as more defensible than broad content bans, particularly in a system that places high value on free expression. The approach recognizes the difficulty of regulating political media while still addressing misuse.

  • Candidates using malicious deepfakes face election disqualification
  • Election Commission supports transparency and traceability approach
  • Disclosure requirements viewed as more defensible than content bans
  • Balances electoral integrity with free expression values

FIELD NOTE

Platform Accountability and Safe Harbor

The amendment significantly alters intermediary liability. Deepfakes are treated on par with real content under IT Act liability provisions, meaning intermediaries cannot argue regulatory gaps to avoid responsibility. The rules affect safe harbor protections, requiring platforms to implement rapid restrictions or suspensions where repeated misuse is detected. Users must be informed every three months about legal consequences of misuse, and violations may result in immediate action. This creates stronger incentives for platforms to proactively manage synthetic content rather than relying on reactive complaints.

  • Deepfakes treated as real content for liability purposes
  • Safe harbor protections affected by new accountability requirements
  • Platforms must implement rapid restrictions for repeated misuse
  • Users informed quarterly about legal consequences of SGI misuse

Closing read

Conclusion

India's 2026 IT Rules Amendment establishes a comprehensive framework for regulating synthetic media in political contexts, with disclosure and rapid takedown as core mechanisms. The rules are effective as of February 20, 2026, and represent a disclosure-based approach rather than content bans, reflecting the challenges of regulating political speech. The framework places significant responsibility on both content creators and platforms, with clear penalties for non-compliance including election disqualification for candidates. While the rules are now in effect, their practical implementation and judicial interpretation remain to be tested, particularly regarding the balance between disclosure requirements and free expression. The approach offers a potential model for other jurisdictions grappling with synthetic media in elections.
