AI Therapy Chatbots Now Required to Disclose Model Version to Patients

New state laws require AI therapy chatbots to disclose their model version and training data to patients, marking a shift from efficacy debates to consumer transparency.

October 27, 2026 · 5:02 AM PDT · 5 min read · 12 sources · Original family: AI in Mental Health & Therapy

Editor’s brief

Why this matters now

As AI chatbots proliferate in mental health care, regulators are moving beyond questions of clinical efficacy to address a more fundamental consumer protection issue: transparency. Emerging state legislation now mandates that AI therapy platforms reveal which model version and training data generated their responses, giving patients insight into the technology they are interacting with. This regulatory shift represents a significant evolution in how AI in healthcare is governed.

This analysis draws on multiple sources tracking state AI healthcare legislation, including compliance guides, legal trackers, and industry analyses. The evidence reveals a complex, evolving regulatory landscape where different states are taking varied approaches to AI therapy oversight, with California, Texas, and Illinois emerging as key regulatory leaders.

FIELD NOTE

The Transparency Mandate

The emerging regulatory trend focuses on requiring AI therapy platforms to provide detailed disclosures about their underlying technology. Rather than simply stating that a service uses AI, new rules mandate specific information about model versions and training data sources. This approach treats AI therapy as a consumer product where informed choice depends on technical transparency.

The rationale behind these disclosure requirements is straightforward: patients deserve to know what they are interacting with. Different model versions may have varying capabilities, biases, or limitations, and training data sources can significantly influence response patterns. By requiring disclosure, regulators aim to empower patients to make informed decisions about their mental health care.

  • Disclosures must specify model version numbers
  • Training data sources must be identified
  • Requirements apply to direct-to-consumer AI therapy platforms
  • Rules distinguish between clinician-assisted and autonomous AI use

State-by-State Regulatory Landscape

The regulatory environment for AI therapy is becoming increasingly fragmented across states. As of October 2026, Texas and California require AI disclosure in healthcare settings, while California specifically bars AI from posing as licensed professionals. Illinois, Nevada, and Utah have implemented restrictions on AI therapy applications, each with different scopes and requirements.

This patchwork of regulations creates compliance challenges for AI therapy companies operating nationally. The Federalist Society's 2026 Chatbot Legislation Tracker notes that nearly 100 chatbot-specific bills were introduced across states in 2026, indicating that the regulatory landscape is still rapidly evolving. Companies must navigate varying disclosure requirements, advertising restrictions, and liability frameworks depending on where they operate.

  • Texas: Requires AI disclosure in healthcare settings

FIELD NOTE

Beyond Disclosure: Broader Regulatory Frameworks

While model version disclosure represents a new frontier, it is part of a broader regulatory framework emerging for AI in mental health. State laws now address multiple aspects of AI therapy, including advertising claims, documentation practices, and safeguards for vulnerable populations such as minors.

Some regulations focus on restricting how AI can be marketed, preventing chatbots from advertising themselves as therapy when they lack clinical validation. Others govern how clinicians can use AI tools in treatment, ensuring that artificial intelligence augments rather than replaces professional judgment. A few states have implemented specific requirements for how AI systems should respond when users express distress or suicidal ideation.

  • Advertising restrictions prevent misleading therapy claims
  • Documentation rules govern AI-generated clinical notes
  • Minor safety provisions address developmental appropriateness
  • Distress response requirements mandate escalation protocols

Closing read

Conclusion

The emergence of AI therapy chatbot disclosure requirements marks a significant evolution in healthcare regulation. Rather than focusing solely on clinical outcomes, regulators are increasingly concerned with transparency and consumer protection. This shift reflects a maturing understanding of AI as a complex technology whose inner workings matter to users, even if those users are not technically sophisticated.

The evidence suggests that this regulatory trend is likely to continue and expand. With nearly 100 chatbot-specific bills introduced in 2026 alone, the regulatory landscape is still coalescing. States that have been early adopters of AI therapy regulations, particularly California, appear to be setting precedents that other states may follow. However, the fragmented nature of state-level regulation creates uncertainty for both providers and patients.

What remains unclear is whether federal regulation will eventually supersede or complement these state-level efforts. The current patchwork approach may prove unsustainable as AI therapy becomes more widespread. Additionally, the effectiveness of disclosure requirements in actually improving patient outcomes or satisfaction remains to be empirically demonstrated. These transparency rules represent a logical first step, but their ultimate impact on the quality and accessibility of AI-assisted mental health care will depend on implementation and enforcement.
