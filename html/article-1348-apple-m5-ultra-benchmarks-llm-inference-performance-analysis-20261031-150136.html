Apple M5 Ultra Benchmarks: LLM Inference Performance Analysis

The M5 Ultra sets a new ceiling for local LLM inference on Apple Silicon, delivering significant throughput gains for large models in a single quiet box.

Editor’s brief

Why this matters now

Apple's M5 Ultra chip, announced for the Mac Studio in August 2026, is rapidly establishing itself as a leading platform for local large language model (LLM) inference. Independent benchmarks and community data suggest substantial performance improvements over previous generations, particularly for large and Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models. This analysis synthesizes available benchmark data, technical specifications, and expert commentary to evaluate the M5 Ultra's real-world capabilities for AI workloads.

Assignment angle: Focuses on the newly released M5 Ultra's specific LLM inference capabilities, providing technical performance data rather than general chip announcements or software updates.

FIELD NOTE

Announcement and Hardware Context

Apple announced the Mac Studio with the M5 Ultra on August 25, 2026. The chip is positioned as a high-end desktop silicon option, with the 512 GB configuration expected to arrive later in the year. The M5 Ultra is a 36-core processor, representing Apple's latest iteration of its custom silicon architecture for professional workloads. It is designed to handle demanding tasks, including local AI inference, without the need for discrete GPUs.

Performance Benchmarks and Throughput

Multiple benchmarking efforts have evaluated the M5 Ultra's LLM inference performance. Community-sourced data and independent measurements indicate that the M5 Ultra is among the fastest quiet single-box machines for large MoE models. Benchmarks show significant improvements in tokens per second compared to previous Apple Silicon chips, particularly for larger models. The performance gains are attributed to increased core count, improved memory bandwidth, and architectural optimizations for AI workloads.

Comparison with Previous Generations

The M5 Ultra demonstrates substantial performance gains over its predecessors. Geekbench 7 tests show a 59% score jump compared to the M3 Ultra, indicating significant improvements in single-threaded and multi-threaded performance. These gains translate to faster LLM inference, particularly for models that benefit from higher computational throughput. The M5 Ultra's performance is expected to surpass the M4 and M4 Pro chips, making it the top choice for local AI workloads on Apple Silicon.

  • 59% Geekbench score improvement over M3 Ultra.
  • Expected to outperform M4 and M4 Pro.
  • Faster inference for computationally intensive models.
  • Improved single-threaded and multi-threaded performance.

Practical Implications for Local AI

The M5 Ultra's performance makes it a compelling option for running large LLMs locally, reducing reliance on cloud-based AI services. With support for models up to 405B parameters, the M5 Ultra can handle some of the most advanced open-source LLMs available. This capability is particularly valuable for developers, researchers, and enterprises seeking to maintain data privacy, reduce latency, and control inference costs. The M5 Ultra's performance also suggests that local AI inference is becoming increasingly viable for production workloads.

  • Supports models up to 405B parameters.
  • Reduces reliance on cloud AI services.
  • Improves data privacy and latency.
  • Viable for production AI workloads.
