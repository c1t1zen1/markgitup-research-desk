Deepfake Laws Now Require Real-Time Disclosure in Live Video Calls

New U.S. and EU deepfake laws now mandate real-time disclosure when synthetic media is used in live video calls, marking a shift from static content to dynamic communication.

The Shift to Live Video Call Disclosure

Traditional deepfake laws focused on pre-recorded videos, images, and audio. However, the rise of AI-powered video conferencing tools and real-time face-swapping technology has prompted legislators to extend disclosure requirements to live calls. The U.S. federal TAKE IT DOWN Act, now in force, establishes a framework for addressing synthetic media in various contexts, including potentially live communications. Meanwhile, the EU's transparency rules, becoming binding in August 2026, are expected to cover real-time synthetic media use. This represents a significant expansion of regulatory scope beyond static content.

How Disclosure Requirements Work in Practice

The specific mechanics of live video call disclosure vary by jurisdiction but generally require clear, immediate notification when synthetic media is being used. In the U.S., compliance guides suggest that disclosure should be conspicuous and occur at the start of the call or when synthetic elements are introduced. The EU's approach emphasizes transparency through standardized labeling and metadata. Businesses are advised to implement technical solutions that automatically flag or watermark synthetic content in real-time. The challenge lies in ensuring disclosure is both timely and unambiguous, especially in fast-paced conversations.
