AI Drafts SEC Filings, But Auditors Still Sign Off

AI drafting tools are accelerating SEC filing preparation, but auditors retain legal responsibility for accuracy, creating a governance gap as hallucination risks grow.

Editor’s brief

Why this matters now

As companies adopt generative AI to draft financial disclosures, a new division of labor is emerging: machines produce the text, but human auditors must still verify and sign off. This split raises questions about liability, especially when AI-generated content contains errors or omissions. The evidence reviewed includes regulatory guidance, practitioner walkthroughs, and industry reports on AI use in audit and SEC compliance.

Assignment angle: Focuses on the emerging split between AI-generated financial disclosures and human auditor liability, a governance question that has surfaced as companies adopt AI drafting tools.

FIELD NOTE

The Emerging Division of Labor

Generative AI tools are increasingly used to draft financial statement footnotes and other SEC filing components. However, these outputs are not final; they require human review and auditor sign-off. This creates a workflow where AI handles initial drafting, while auditors focus on verification and compliance checks.

  • AI tools can significantly reduce drafting time for complex disclosures
  • Auditors remain legally responsible for the accuracy of filed content
  • The split between drafting and verification is a new governance challenge

FIELD NOTE

Hallucination Risks in Financial Disclosures

One of the primary concerns with AI-generated financial disclosures is the risk of hallucination—where the model generates plausible but incorrect information. In the context of SEC filings, this could mean inaccurate numbers, misstated facts, or incorrect regulatory references. The stakes are higher for financial statement footnotes, which carry audited numbers and specific disclosure requirements.

  • AI hallucinations can introduce factual errors into filings
  • Footnote disclosures are particularly vulnerable due to their technical nature
  • Errors may not be caught during initial review, leading to enforcement actions

Regulatory Scrutiny and Compliance

Regulators are increasingly aware of AI's role in financial reporting. The SEC has issued guidance on AI disclosure risks, and other bodies like FINRA are monitoring trends in AI use. Companies must ensure their AI-generated content meets regulatory standards and is properly disclosed.

  • SEC guidance emphasizes the need for accurate AI-generated disclosures
  • FINRA is tracking AI use in financial services for regulatory oversight
  • Companies face potential enforcement actions for non-compliance

Conclusion

The use of generative AI in SEC filings is transforming the drafting process, but it also introduces new risks and governance challenges. While AI can improve efficiency, the responsibility for accuracy remains with human auditors. As AI tools evolve, so too will the regulatory landscape, requiring companies to stay vigilant and adapt their compliance strategies accordingly.
