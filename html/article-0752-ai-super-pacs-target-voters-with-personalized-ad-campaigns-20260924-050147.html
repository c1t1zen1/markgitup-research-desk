AI Super PACs Target Voters With Personalized Ad Campaigns

AI super PACs are spending tens of millions on hyper-targeted digital ads in the 2026 midterms, using voter data to shape narratives around artificial intelligence policy.

September 24, 2026 · 5:01 AM PDT · 4 min read · 12 sources · Original family: AI in US Elections & Campaigns

Editor’s brief

Why this matters now

This article examines how artificial intelligence-focused super PACs have emerged as major financial and strategic players in the 2026 US midterm elections. Drawing on reporting from WIRED, POLITICO, The Guardian, and other outlets, along with spending data from OpenSecrets and ad-tracking firm AdImpact, the evidence shows a coordinated effort by tech industry-aligned groups to influence congressional races. The coverage reveals both the scale of spending and the specific targeting strategies employed against individual candidates.

Assignment angle: This angle focuses on the real-time microtargeting capabilities of AI-driven political ad campaigns, distinct from prior coverage of ballot scanners, deepfake disclosure, or training data legislation.

FIELD NOTE

The Scale of AI Super PAC Spending

AI-focused political action committees have invested at least $44 million into 40 congressional candidates as of the end of June 2026, according to CNBC reporting cited by The Political Group. OpenSecrets data confirms $43.3 million in AI super PAC spending on congressional races during this cycle. WIRED reported that AI PACs had dumped nearly $1 million into a single obscure Senate race, illustrating the concentrated nature of some spending. The Tech Oversight Project has cataloged more than 175 AI-related political ads this year, according to a June statement by its director Sacha Haworth. An AI super PAC ad library maintained by the Tech Oversight Project lists 10 PACs running ads as of August 20, 2026.

FIELD NOTE

Targeting Specific Candidates and Narratives

AI super PACs have deployed highly targeted ad campaigns against individual candidates. In California, super PACs tied to AIPAC spent more than $5 million in recent weeks on ads targeting progressive candidate Aisha Wahab and supporting Melissa Hernandez, according to The Guardian. In New York, congressional candidate Alex Bores faced a digital ad campaign led by a Leading the Future affiliate that attacked his previous position at Palantir Technologies, despite his anti-ICE stance, according to Scot Scoop News. In efforts to topple Senator Sherrod Brown of Ohio, Fairshake ads focused on the southern border and China, while campaigns against California progressive Katie Porter attempted to tar her with unfavorable associations, according to The Outpost. In Texas, the American Mission super PAC accounted for nearly half of all ad spending on one candidate's behalf during the primary, according to AdImpact data.

  • Over $5 million spent in California targeting Aisha Wahab
  • Alex Bores targeted over Palantir employment despite anti-ICE position
  • Fairshake ads against Sherrod Brown focused on border and China
  • American Mission accounted for nearly half of ad spending in one Texas primary

Tech Industry Donors and Political Alignment

The Andreessen Horowitz firm, known as a16z, emerged as the biggest single donor in the 2026 elections, according to a May New York Times report cited by POLITICO. However, other tech billionaires have remained loyal to Democrats, creating fractures within the industry's political giving. Democratic House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries has called for Congressional action to regulate artificial intelligence, appearing on ABC's Sunday morning show to discuss the party's approach to AI policy. The interplay between industry donors and party platforms has become a defining feature of the election cycle, with AI increasingly becoming a campaign issue rather than just a policy matter.

Closing read

Conclusion

The evidence clearly establishes that AI super PACs have become significant financial actors in the 2026 midterm elections, with tens of millions of dollars spent on targeted advertising campaigns. The spending is documented by multiple independent sources including OpenSecrets, AdImpact, and the Tech Oversight Project. The targeting of specific candidates with tailored narratives is well-documented across multiple states and races. However, projections about the long-term impact of this spending on election outcomes or AI policy remain speculative. The Tech Oversight Project's characterization of this spending as an effort to 'write the rules in their favor' represents an interpretation of motive rather than established fact.
