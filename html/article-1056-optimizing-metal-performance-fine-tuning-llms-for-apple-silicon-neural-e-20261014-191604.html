LLMs are advanced AI systems, typically neural networks, trained on massive text datasets to process and generate language. Apple Silicon hardware is noted for its capability to run LLMs locally, utilizing features such as unified memory and Metal support. Performance Benchmarks: MLX vs. llama.cpp vs. Metal

The performance comparison between different local LLM stacks on Apple Silicon is a key area of current research. Historically, llama.cpp lagged behind MLX in raw throughput when mapping operations through the Metal Performance Primitives layer, as opposed to direct Metal API calls. However, this disparity has reportedly narrowed significantly between late 2025 and early 2026.

Specific updates, such as llama.cpp merging support for the Metal 4 Tensor API, indicate ongoing hardware-software integration improvements. Furthermore, for certain model sizes and quantizations, MLX has been observed to outperform llama.cpp on Apple hardware.

  • Historically, llama.cpp lagged MLX in raw throughput on Apple Silicon due to its reliance on the Metal Performance Primitives layer.
  • The gap between MLX and llama.cpp narrowed significantly between late 2025 and early 2026.
  • llama.cpp incorporated support for the Metal 4 Tensor API.

Conclusion

In summary, deploying LLMs on Apple Silicon is a highly specialized endeavor, moving beyond general AI toolkits to focus intensely on hardware-specific optimizations like Metal and the Neural Engine. While MLX and llama.cpp show competitive performance, the specific tuning required to extract peak performance—especially concerning Metal API utilization—remains critical.

Forecasts suggest that the performance gaps between these stacks will continue to narrow as software integrations, such as Metal 4 Tensor API support, become more prevalent. Users must carefully select their deployment stack based on whether their priority is raw throughput, low latency, or ease of setup.

Ultimately, success hinges on understanding the nuances of the underlying hardware capabilities and matching the right inference engine (like lithos-metal or MLX) to the specific LLM model and desired application.
