AI Drafts First SEC Filings for Public Companies

AI systems are moving from analyzing to autonomously drafting SEC filings, a shift that could reshape financial reporting but faces accuracy and regulatory hurdles.

September 25, 2026 · 11:01 PM PDT · 5 min read · 12 sources · Original family: Generative AI for Financial Reporting
AIfinancial reportingSEC filingsautomation
Editor’s brief

Why this matters now

The evidence shows AI is deeply embedded in SEC filing workflows, primarily for analysis, summarization, and risk identification. Multiple tools use large language models to process 10-K and 10-Q documents, extract key information, and compare disclosures across periods. The SEC itself has been actively engaged with AI-related disclosure issues, issuing 92 comment letters to 56 companies since 2021. While the technology demonstrates strong potential for automating routine reporting tasks, benchmarks reveal significant accuracy challenges when moving from single-document analysis to cross-entity and temporal comparisons.

Assignment angle: Focuses on AI moving from assisting to autonomously drafting regulatory financial disclosures, a concrete milestone in financial reporting automation.

Current State of AI in SEC Filing Workflows

AI systems are already widely deployed for analyzing SEC filings, with tools capable of identifying risk factors, summarizing MD&A sections, and comparing year-over-year disclosures. Platforms like V7 Go offer specialized agents for 10-K and 10-Q analysis, while others focus on sentiment scoring and market impact assessment of filings. FIELD NOTE

Accuracy Benchmarks and Technical Limitations

Independent benchmarks provide insight into AI performance on SEC filing tasks. One benchmark reported GPT-4 achieving approximately 81.5% accuracy on a synthetic 10-K question-answering task, outperforming other models that scored between 60-70%. However, a newer benchmark called Fin-RATE, published in February 2026, evaluated 17 large language models across document-retrieval, enterprise-comparison, and longitudinal-tracking tasks. This benchmark found that accuracy drops by 14-19% when moving from single-document reasoning to cross-entity and temporal analysis, with comparison hallucinations and entity mismatches identified as primary drivers of errors.

  • GPT-4 achieved ~81.5% accuracy on synthetic 10-K QA benchmark
  • Other models scored 60-70% on the same benchmark
  • Fin-RATE benchmark (February 2026) tested 17 LLMs on SEC filings
  • Accuracy drops 14-19% for cross-entity and temporal analysis tasks

Regulatory Scrutiny and Compliance Considerations

The SEC has been actively monitoring AI-related disclosures, issuing 92 separate AI-related comment letters to 56 companies since 2021. The agency has also charged two firms under AI washing enforcement actions. In February 2026, the SEC received a formal petition for mandatory AI governance disclosure rules. This regulatory environment suggests that companies using AI to draft or analyze filings will face increasing scrutiny regarding the accuracy, transparency, and governance of their AI systems.

Closing read

Conclusion

The evidence indicates AI is well-established in SEC filing analysis but faces significant challenges in autonomous drafting. While accuracy on single-document tasks is promising (81.5% for GPT-4 on synthetic benchmarks), the 14-19% accuracy drop on cross-entity and temporal analysis tasks suggests AI is not yet ready to fully replace human drafters for complex regulatory filings. The SEC's active engagement with AI disclosure issues, including 92 comment letters and enforcement actions, indicates a regulatory environment that will demand high standards for AI-generated content. The formal petition for mandatory AI governance disclosure rules filed in February 2026 suggests this scrutiny will likely intensify.
