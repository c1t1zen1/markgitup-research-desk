Pentagon Tests AI That Predicts Adversary Moves in Real-Time

The U.S. Department of War is accelerating AI integration for battlefield decision-making, with new systems designed to deliver real-time tactical options to commanders amid ongoing oversight challenges.

Editor’s brief

Why this matters now

Recent reporting and official documents indicate a significant shift in how the U.S. military is approaching artificial intelligence, moving from experimental projects to operational deployment focused on combat decision-making. The Department of War has identified seven "Pace-Setting Projects" under its AI Acceleration Strategy, with particular emphasis on systems that can process battlefield data and provide commanders with actionable options in real-time. This represents a distinct evolution from earlier AI efforts that focused primarily on analysis and prediction to systems designed for active integration into the command and control cycle. The evidence gathered from federal news outlets, official Pentagon documents, and defense industry reporting suggests both ambitious progress and persistent institutional challenges in this transformation.

Assignment angle: Focuses on the U.S. military's emerging use of AI for real-time predictive analysis of enemy tactics, a distinct application from civilian surveillance or commercial intelligence gathering.

FIELD NOTE

From Analysis to Decision Support

The Pentagon's AI strategy has evolved from analytical tools to decision-support systems integrated directly into military operations. According to a policy memo reviewed by Bloomberg, the Department intends to "accelerate AI-enabled decision-making and put it at the heart of its ambition to link up sensors and shooters for combat operations." This represents a fundamental shift in how AI is conceptualized within the military - not as a separate analytical tool but as an integral component of the operational decision-making process itself.

  • AI systems are being designed to connect sensor data directly to weapon systems and tactical decisions
  • The focus has shifted from post-hoc analysis to real-time decision support during operations
  • Military leaders are exploring how to integrate AI into the command and control cycle

The Agent Network and Pace-Setting Projects

One of the most significant developments is the Agent Network project, identified as one of seven Pace-Setting Projects under the Department's AI Acceleration Strategy. According to reporting from the Potomac Officers Club, this system "will deploy AI agents to deliver battle decision options to commanders," suggesting a networked approach where multiple AI systems work together to provide comprehensive tactical recommendations. The Department of War's official AI strategy document confirms this approach, stating that resources will be focused on "unlocking critical foundational enablers needed to accelerate war-winning efforts across the Department, starting with enabling the set of seven PSPs listed below in fiscal year 2026."

  • Agent Network represents a networked approach to AI decision support
  • Seven Pace-Setting Projects have been identified as priorities for fiscal year 2026
  • The Chief Digital and AI Office (CDAO) is being re-focused to support these initiatives
  • Projects address opportunities across Warfighting, Intelligence, and Enterprise functions

Oversight and Implementation Challenges

Despite the ambitious strategy, significant challenges remain in implementing and overseeing these AI systems. A Government Accountability Office report found that the Pentagon office responsible for overseeing responsible AI use had filled only three of ten authorized positions as of February 2026, according to Defense News. This staffing shortage raises questions about the Department's ability to maintain adequate oversight as AI systems become more deeply integrated into military operations. The rapid pace of deployment appears to be outstripping the development of governance structures designed to ensure these systems function as intended.

  • The AI oversight office was significantly understaffed as of early 2026
  • Only 3 of 10 authorized positions had been filled according to GAO findings
  • The pace of AI deployment appears to be exceeding oversight capacity
  • Questions remain about safeguards accompanying advanced decision-support systems

The Decision Advantage Concept

Underlying these technological developments is the military concept of "decision advantage" - the idea that superior decision-making speed and quality can overcome other tactical disadvantages. Military Times reported on this concept, noting that while "drones, artificial intelligence and advanced battlefield networks may transform the mechanics of war," the fundamental requirement for effective leadership remains. This suggests that AI systems are being developed not to replace human commanders but to enhance their decision-making capabilities by providing faster, more comprehensive analysis of battlefield conditions.

  • Decision advantage is a key strategic concept driving AI adoption
  • AI is intended to augment rather than replace human commanders
  • The goal is faster, more informed tactical decisions during operations
  • Leadership remains essential even as technology transforms warfare

Closing read

Conclusion

The evidence suggests the U.S. military is undergoing a significant transformation in how it approaches artificial intelligence, moving from experimental projects to operational systems designed for real-time battlefield decision support. The Department of War's AI Acceleration Strategy and its seven Pace-Setting Projects represent a coordinated effort to integrate AI into the core of military operations, with particular emphasis on connecting sensor data to tactical decisions. However, the pace of this transformation appears to be creating challenges, particularly in terms of oversight and governance. The understaffed AI oversight office identified by the GAO suggests that institutional capacity may not be keeping pace with technological deployment. While the strategic vision is clear - achieving "decision advantage" through AI-enhanced command and control - the implementation remains a work in progress with significant challenges ahead.
