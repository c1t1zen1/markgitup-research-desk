FIELD NOTE

EU AI Act and Deepfake Transparency Obligations

The EU AI Act establishes transparency obligations under Article 50 for content that is artificially generated or manipulated, including deepfakes. These obligations require disclosure that the content is synthetic. Enforcement of these specific deepfake labelling rules has been enforceable since August 2, 2026. There is also mention of a marking deadline of December 2, 2026, and potential fines up to 3% of global turnover for general-purpose AI providers.

  • Transparency obligations apply to deepfake and AI-generated content under Article 50.
  • Enforcement of these obligations began on August 2, 2026.
  • Fines for general-purpose AI providers can reach 3% of global turnover.

FIELD NOTE

EU Governance and Implementation Structure

The EU operates as a supranational union of 27 member states, with the European Commission serving as the main executive body. The enforcement of the AI Act is not centralized solely within the Commission; it is shared across the AI Office, the European Data Protection Supervisor, and national competent authorities appointed by the Member States. This shared enforcement structure within the EU itself highlights the involvement of national bodies in EU-level regulation.

  • The EU comprises 27 member states.
  • Enforcement involves the AI Office, EDPB, and national competent authorities.
  • The European Commission proposes legislation and upholds EU treaties.

FIELD NOTE

Timeline and Scope of the EU AI Act

The EU AI Act is described as the world's first comprehensive AI law, applying in tiers. Prohibited practices were set for February 2025, GPAI rules for August 2025, and high-risk AI obligations are scheduled for later dates, specifically December 2027 and August 2028, following 2026 amendments. The Act moved from drafting to enforcement on August 2, 2026.

  • The EU AI Act is the world's first comprehensive AI law.
  • Prohibited practices apply from February 2025.
  • High-risk AI obligations are set for December 2027 and August 2028.

Conclusion

The evidence confirms the EU has implemented specific, enforceable rules regarding deepfake transparency via the AI Act, with enforcement shared among EU bodies and national authorities. However, the provided sources focus exclusively on the EU framework and do not offer comparative data on how other national digital acts implement or conflict with this standard.
