FIELD NOTE

The liability question is moving upstream

Most state deepfake laws reportedly penalize the person who creates or shares nonconsensual content. Source 2 presents Minnesota's HF 1606 as a notable departure because it would also hold the AI platform liable when the platform is used to generate that content, regardless of intent or knowledge, with an effective date of August 1, 2026.

That distinction changes the legal target. A creator-focused model asks who made or distributed the material; a platform-focused model asks what responsibility attaches to the service that enabled generation. The supplied evidence does not establish how Minnesota's rule would be enforced in practice or whether it creates a removal deadline, so any claim that it guarantees rapid takedown would exceed the record.

Closing read

Conclusion

The evidence supports a clear directional finding: state deepfake policy is not limited to punishing creators, and at least one supplied account describes Minnesota HF 1606 as extending liability to an AI platform regardless of intent or knowledge (Source 2). Other sources describe a fragmented field involving explicit deepfakes, civil and criminal remedies, private rights, minors, and a claimed federal baseline (Sources 6, 8, 10).

The stronger claim—that 2026 state rules impose uniform, rapid takedown deadlines and clearly assign enforcement responsibility—is not established by the snippets. That is the key unresolved issue. Forecast: as more provisions take effect or are interpreted, litigation and legislative guidance may determine whether platform liability produces fast removal, procedural safeguards, or conflicting obligations across states.
