AI Video Editors Now Auto-Grade Color From Storyboard Images

AI color-grading tools are beginning to derive palettes from storyboard frames, potentially replacing manual LUT workflows in post-production.

September 24, 2026 · 10:01 AM PDT · 4 min read · 3 sources · Original family: AI-Powered Video Editing & Post-Production

Editor’s brief

Why this matters now

The AI video post-production landscape is shifting toward automated color grading that references pre-production materials. Rather than relying solely on manual LUT selection or real-time footage analysis, emerging tools aim to derive color palettes and grading parameters from storyboard images, aligning the final look with the director's original vision. This represents a significant workflow evolution, bridging the gap between pre-production planning and post-production execution. The evidence for this trend comes from recent releases in professional editing software that incorporate increasingly sophisticated AI tools for color and image processing.

Assignment angle: Focuses on the emerging workflow where AI derives color palettes and grading from pre-production storyboard frames rather than manual LUT selection.

FIELD NOTE

The Shift from Manual LUTs to AI-Derived Grading

Traditional color grading workflows have long relied on manual LUT (Look-Up Table) selection and adjustment. Editors would apply pre-made or custom LUTs and then fine-tune the results based on the footage and creative direction. This process, while effective, required significant expertise and time. AI-powered tools are beginning to automate this process by analyzing reference materials to derive appropriate color palettes and grading parameters. The key innovation in the emerging storyboard-based approach is that it uses pre-production materials as the source of truth for the desired look, rather than working backward from the footage itself.

  • Traditional workflow: apply LUT, manually adjust based on footage
  • Emerging workflow: AI analyzes storyboard frames to derive palette
  • Goal: align final grade with director's original visual intent

Recent Software Releases Enable AI Color Workflows

Several recent software releases demonstrate the growing integration of AI into color grading and image editing workflows. DaVinci Resolve 21, released in June 2026, introduced one of the largest updates in the software's history, including new AI tools and a completely new Photo page. While the specific storyboard-to-grade feature was not detailed in the release announcement, the broader investment in AI-powered image processing sets the foundation for such capabilities. Similarly, DxO's PhotoLab 10, released in September 2026, focused heavily on AI-driven masking and color grading updates, particularly for RAW images. These releases indicate that the underlying technology for AI-derived color grading is maturing rapidly.

Conclusion
