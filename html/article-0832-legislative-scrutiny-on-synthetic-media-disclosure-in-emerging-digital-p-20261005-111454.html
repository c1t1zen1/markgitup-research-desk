Legislative Scrutiny on Synthetic Media Disclosure in Emerging Digital Platforms

State-level mandates are creating a complex legal patchwork as jurisdictions enforce disclosure rules for synthetic media in advertising.

Editor’s brief

Why this matters now

This article examines the emerging trend of state-level legislative action targeting the use of synthetic media, specifically focusing on disclosure requirements for AI-generated content in advertising. The evidence gathered highlights specific laws enacted in California and New York that mandate labeling for synthetic performers and AI-generated audio/visuals. The analysis centers on how these varied state mandates are influencing the compliance challenges facing digital platforms attempting to standardize their operations across different jurisdictions.

Assignment angle: This angle focuses on the evolving patchwork of state-level disclosure mandates for synthetic content as platforms attempt to standardize compliance.

FIELD NOTE

California's Mandate for AI Voice and Visual Disclosure

California has established specific requirements for synthetic content. SB 1050 mandates that advertisers must label both AI-generated audio and visual performances. This law was signed by Governor Gavin Newsom, requiring clear disclosure when synthetic performers are used in advertisements. The enactment of this law contributes to a growing collection of regulations concerning AI in the state.

  • California's SB 1050 requires labeling of AI-generated audio and visual performances.
  • Governor Gavin Newsom signed the law requiring disclosure of synthetic performers in ads.

New York's Requirement for Synthetic Performer Disclosure

New York has also introduced legislation addressing synthetic content in advertising. The state enacted a law requiring advertisers to conspicuously disclose the use of 'synthetic performers.' These performers can be generated using AI or other software algorithms. Legal discussions surrounding this law detail the new disclosure obligations for AI-generated performers in advertising and film.

  • New York requires advertisers to conspicuously disclose the use of 'synthetic performers.'
  • The disclosure requirement applies to performers generated using AI or other software algorithms.

The Patchwork Effect and Broader Context

These state-level requirements—in California and New York—demonstrate the development of a fragmented regulatory landscape. This patchwork presents a challenge for digital platforms seeking uniformity in compliance. Furthermore, the issue extends beyond advertising; reports indicate that AI-generated campaign advertisements have appeared in recent political cycles, with some lacking disclosure.

  • State laws are contributing to a growing patchwork of AI regulations.
  • Lack of disclosure has been noted in campaign advertisements using AI in the 2026 midterms.

Closing read

Conclusion

The evidence clearly demonstrates that states like California and New York are actively creating specific, mandatory disclosure rules for synthetic media used in advertising. These laws are establishing concrete compliance hurdles for entities operating across these jurisdictions. However, the evidence also suggests that despite some state efforts, the lack of disclosure persists in other contexts, such as political advertising, indicating that current legislation has not fully curtailed the issue.

Forecasts suggest that this state-level proliferation will force platforms to develop complex, jurisdiction-specific compliance mechanisms to manage the varied mandates. The current situation is characterized by localized regulatory action rather than a unified federal standard.
