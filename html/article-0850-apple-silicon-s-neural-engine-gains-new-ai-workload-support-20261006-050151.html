Apple Silicon's Neural Engine Gains New AI Workload Support

Apple's Neural Engine is evolving from a mobile feature into the core of its hardware strategy, driving new Mac and iPhone performance while exposing early reliability challenges.

Why this matters now

This article examines how Apple's Neural Engine has become a central focus of its silicon roadmap, with implications for local AI workloads on macOS. The evidence shows a clear trajectory: from the A20 Pro's 32-core Neural Engine in the iPhone 18 Pro to the M7 Ultra's planned 1.5TB memory support, Apple is engineering its chips specifically for AI workloads. FIELD NOTE

The A20 Pro's Neural Engine Architecture

Apple's A20 Pro chip, announced September 9, 2026, features a 32-core Neural Engine built on 2-nanometer process technology. The chip delivers what Apple claims is 50 percent faster AI processing compared to its predecessor. The A20 Pro powers the iPhone 18 Pro, iPhone 18 Pro Max, and Apple's first foldable smartphone, the iPhone Duo.

The Neural Engine in the A20 Pro represents a significant architectural shift. Apple quotes the dual 16-core configuration at double the AI processing throughput of previous generations. This hardware is separate from the chip's other AI accelerators, which do not carry a core count designation.

  • 32-core Neural Engine on 2nm process technology
  • Powers iPhone 18 Pro, Pro Max, and iPhone Duo
  • Apple claims 50% faster AI processing vs. predecessor
  • Separate from other AI accelerators in the chip

Neural Engine Reliability Issues Emerge

Despite the architectural advances, Apple's newest devices have experienced early reliability problems tied to the Neural Engine. Apple Watch Series 12 and Apple Watch Ultra 4 models have reported Neural Engine timeouts that crash the devices. A smaller group of iPhone 18 Pro owners have experienced Face ID panics, also attributed to Neural Engine issues.

These problems suggest that the transition to more complex AI hardware has introduced new failure modes. The Neural Engine's increased role in core device functions like biometric authentication makes these issues particularly noticeable to users.

  • Neural Engine timeouts crashing Apple Watch Series 12 and Ultra 4
  • Face ID panics reported by iPhone 18 Pro owners
  • Issues attributed to Neural Engine hardware or firmware
  • Problems emerged shortly after device launches

FIELD NOTE

Mac Silicon AI Roadmap: M6 to M7 Ultra

Apple's Mac lineup has received significant AI-focused updates. The new Mac mini M6 and Mac Studio M5 Ultra are now on sale, with the M5 Ultra featuring quad-die processing power. The M6 chip replaces the M4 and M4 Pro models that had been on sale since October 2024.

Looking ahead, Bloomberg's Mark Gurman reported that Apple is engineering the M7 Ultra to support up to 1.5 terabytes of unified memory, roughly double the ceiling planned for the M6 Ultra. This memory expansion is specifically targeted at AI workloads, with Apple reportedly eyeing NVIDIA's Blackwell architecture as competition.

  • M6 and M5 Ultra now shipping in Mac mini and Mac Studio
  • M5 Ultra features quad-die processing architecture
  • M7 Ultra planned with up to 1.5TB unified memory
  • Memory expansion targeted at AI workloads

macOS 27 and the End of Intel Support

Apple's commitment to its Neural Engine strategy is further evidenced by the upcoming end of Intel Mac support. At WWDC 2026, Apple will officially announce that macOS 27 will require Apple Silicon, leaving the final four Intel Mac models permanently unsupported. This decision is directly tied to the Neural Engine's role in modern macOS AI features.

The transition marks a complete shift to Apple's custom silicon across its entire Mac lineup, enabling consistent Neural Engine performance and features across all Mac devices.

  • macOS 27 will require Apple Silicon
  • Four final Intel Mac models will lose support
  • Decision driven by Neural Engine integration
  • Announcement scheduled for WWDC 2026 keynote

Closing read

Conclusion

Apple's Neural Engine has evolved from a mobile AI feature into the foundation of its hardware strategy across iPhone, Apple Watch, and Mac platforms. The A20 Pro's 32-core Neural Engine and the planned M7 Ultra's 1.5TB memory support demonstrate Apple's commitment to hardware-accelerated AI workloads. The end of Intel Mac support with macOS 27 further cements this direction.

However, the early reliability issues on Apple Watch and iPhone 18 Pro devices suggest that the transition is not without challenges. As the Neural Engine takes on more critical functions, from biometric authentication to complex AI processing, maintaining stability will be essential for user confidence.
