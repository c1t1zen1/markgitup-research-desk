FIELD NOTE

Defining the Sovereignty Landscape

The concept of sovereignty in the digital age is multifaceted. Legal definitions distinguish between 'data sovereignty' (control over access to data, including foreign-state access), 'digital sovereignty' (control over digital infrastructure and capabilities), and 'AI sovereignty' (the legal, operational, and technological capacity to develop AI). Governments view data sovereignty as preserving their decision-making power over their data.

AI itself is described as technology enabling machines to simulate human learning, while data sovereignty for enterprise AI involves legal obligations, technical architecture, and trade-offs when deploying large language models (LLMs) at scale. The discussion around AI sovereignty has entered policy discussions as governments confront the strategic importance of AI infrastructure and data.

  • Data sovereignty relates to legal and operational control over data access.
  • Digital sovereignty pertains to control over digital infrastructure.
  • AI sovereignty involves the capacity to legally and technologically develop AI.
