AI Trading Firms Face First Antitrust Probe Over Shared Models

FTC opens industry-wide probe into AI labs over consumer risks and potential unfair practices, raising antitrust questions for firms using shared foundational models.

Editor’s brief

Why this matters now

On September 30, 2026, the Federal Trade Commission confirmed a sweeping probe into OpenAI, Anthropic, and other AI firms, examining potential consumer harms, unfair or deceptive practices, and the dangers posed by emerging AI technologies. The investigation comes a day after President Trump signed a non-binding self-regulation accord with AI industry leaders, suggesting regulators are moving beyond voluntary frameworks. Multiple sources confirm the FTC is seeking documents from the companies and has already begun questioning industry rivals in related antitrust matters. The probe's timing and scope suggest regulators are increasingly concerned about market concentration and the potential for coordinated behavior among firms relying on similar foundational AI models.

Assignment angle: Regulators investigate whether hedge funds using the same foundational LLMs are creating de facto collusion in market-making strategies.

FIELD NOTE

The FTC Probe: Scope and Significance

The Federal Trade Commission's investigation represents a significant escalation in regulatory scrutiny of the AI industry. According to Reuters, a senior FTC official described the probe as industry-wide, targeting not just individual companies but the broader ecosystem of AI development and deployment. The New York Post reports the FTC is specifically looking into allegations of unfair or deceptive acts or practices that may violate the FTC Act. CNBC notes the probe adds to mounting scrutiny that OpenAI and Anthropic have already been facing over their safety practices, particularly after industry researchers warned about potential risks from the companies' AI models. The investigation's breadth suggests regulators are concerned about systemic issues rather than isolated incidents.

Antitrust Implications for Shared AI Models

While the initial FTC announcement focused on consumer protection, the probe's scope raises significant antitrust questions, particularly for hedge funds and trading firms that rely on shared foundational AI models. Tech Insider reports that the FTC has already widened its Microsoft antitrust probe to include cloud bundling, Entra ID, and Copilot AI, questioning at least six rivals in 2026. This suggests regulators are increasingly attentive to how AI technologies are integrated into broader business practices and market structures. If multiple trading firms use the same foundational models from OpenAI or Anthropic, regulators may investigate whether this creates de facto coordination in market-making strategies, potentially reducing competition and innovation.

Conclusion

The FTC's industry-wide probe into AI firms represents a pivotal moment for the artificial intelligence industry and its applications in financial markets. The investigation's focus on consumer harms, unfair practices, and potential dangers from AI technologies suggests regulators are moving beyond theoretical concerns to examine real-world impacts. For hedge funds and trading firms, the probe raises important questions about the competitive dynamics created by shared foundational AI models. As the investigation progresses, trading firms should monitor developments closely and consider how their AI adoption strategies align with evolving regulatory expectations.
