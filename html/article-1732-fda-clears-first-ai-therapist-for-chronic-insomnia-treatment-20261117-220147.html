FDA Clears First AI Therapist for Chronic Insomnia Treatment

FDA clearance of Somryst marks a regulatory milestone for AI-guided digital therapeutics in treating chronic insomnia, shifting mental health care toward clinically validated software.

November 17, 2026 · 10:01 PM PST · 4 min read · 12 sources · Original family: AI in Mental Health & Therapy

Editor’s brief

Why this matters now

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration has cleared the first prescription-based digital therapeutic for chronic insomnia in adults, signaling a new era for AI-powered mental health treatments. Somryst, developed by Pear Therapeutics and formerly known as SHUTi, uses cognitive behavioral therapy and data-based sleep restrictions to address insomnia, representing a departure from traditional app-based wellness tools toward clinically validated digital interventions. This clearance builds on the FDA's evolving regulatory framework for digital therapeutics, which has included guidance on prescription drug apps and software to support innovation while ensuring safety and efficacy. The approval process for digital therapeutics differs from traditional drug approvals, focusing on software validation and clinical evidence of therapeutic benefit rather than chemical composition and pharmacokinetics.

Assignment angle: Regulatory milestone where an AI-powered digital therapeutic receives formal FDA clearance for a specific mental health condition, marking a shift from app-based tools to clinically validated AI treatment.

How Somryst Works: AI-Guided CBT-I

Somryst employs cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia (CBT-I), the gold-standard non-pharmacological treatment for chronic insomnia, delivered through an AI-guided digital platform. The therapy combines traditional CBT-I techniques with data-based sleep restrictions, using patient input and behavioral data to personalize treatment protocols. The AI component analyzes sleep patterns, treatment response, and patient-reported outcomes to adapt the therapy in real-time, potentially improving efficacy compared to static digital programs. This approach addresses a key limitation of traditional CBT-I: accessibility. Many patients struggle to find qualified CBT-I therapists, and the digital format removes geographic and scheduling barriers while maintaining therapeutic rigor.

Closing read

Conclusion

The FDA's clearance of Somryst represents a genuine regulatory milestone for AI-powered mental health treatments, validating the potential of digital therapeutics to deliver clinically effective interventions. The evidence supports that this clearance establishes a precedent for future digital therapeutics and addresses real accessibility challenges in mental health care delivery. However, it is important to distinguish between the regulatory achievement and the broader claims sometimes made about AI in mental health. Somryst's clearance validates one specific approach to one specific condition; it does not prove that AI will replace human therapists or that all digital mental health tools will achieve similar regulatory success. The long-term impact will depend on clinical outcomes, cost-effectiveness, and how well these tools integrate into existing healthcare systems.
