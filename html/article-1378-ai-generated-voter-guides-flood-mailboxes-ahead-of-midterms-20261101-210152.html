AI-Generated Voter Guides Flood Mailboxes Ahead of Midterms

AI chatbots are increasingly used as voter guides, but tests reveal frequent errors including invented races and misidentified candidates, raising concerns about election integrity.

Why this matters now

As the 2026 midterm elections approach, AI chatbots like ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude are being consulted by voters for election information and ballot guidance. A September survey by the American Association of Political Consultants Foundation found that about one in five voters now use AI chatbots for election news and voting guidance, with Democrats and Republicans using them at similar rates. However, rigorous testing reveals significant accuracy problems that could affect voter decisions.

The evidence combines survey data, systematic testing, and journalistic investigation to assess both the growing role of AI in voter information and the reliability of that information.

Growing Voter Reliance on AI Chatbots

AI chatbots have become ad hoc voter guides for a significant portion of the electorate ahead of the 2026 midterms. According to a September survey by the American Association of Political Consultants Foundation, approximately one in five voters now consult AI chatbots for election news and voting guidance. The survey found that Democrats and Republicans use these tools at similar rates, suggesting the trend crosses partisan lines.

NPR has profiled individual voters who have leaned on ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude to work through their 2026 midterm ballots. This represents a shift from traditional voter information sources such as official voter guides, news organizations, and campaign materials to conversational AI interfaces that voters can query directly about their specific ballot.

Testing Reveals Significant Accuracy Problems

The Washington Post conducted systematic testing of six chatbots on more than 3,000 voter guides, generating AI-created guides for addresses in every county in the United States. The analysis found that the chatbots frequently made errors, including inventing races that do not exist and misidentifying candidates running in actual races.

Errors in the AI-generated voter guides were traced to AI-built sites like Ballot Brief, which themselves may contain inaccuracies that chatbots then propagate. The testing methodology involved asking chatbots to create voter guides for real addresses, then comparing the AI-generated information against actual ballot data to identify discrepancies.

  • Six chatbots tested on 3,000+ voter guides across all U.S. counties
  • Chatbots invented races that do not exist on actual ballots
  • Candidates were frequently misidentified in AI-generated guides
  • Errors traced to AI-built information sites like Ballot Brief

Research Efforts to Monitor AI Election Responses

Academic institutions have begun developing tools to track and analyze how AI chatbots respond to election-related questions. MIT researchers have built the LLM Election Observatory, a dashboard that examines in real-time how popular AI chatbots are responding to questions about the 2026 midterm elections.

This monitoring effort reflects growing recognition that AI systems are becoming significant sources of election information for voters. The observatory provides a way to systematically assess the accuracy and consistency of AI-generated election information over time, rather than relying on one-time tests.

  • MIT's LLM Election Observatory monitors AI chatbot responses in real-time
  • Focuses on questions about the 2026 midterm elections
  • Provides systematic tracking of AI election information accuracy
  • Represents academic effort to understand AI's role in voter information

Broader AI Integration in Election Information Ecosystem

The 2026 midterms represent the first elections in which artificial intelligence is integrated into every major social media platform. This broader integration means that AI is affecting not just direct voter queries to chatbots, but also the election information environment more generally through content recommendation, ad targeting, and information curation.

Voters are encountering a growing stream of AI-generated political ads, images, audio, and video alongside the chatbot voter guides. The efforts to address the potential harms of AI in elections have been described as uneven, suggesting that the infrastructure for ensuring AI-generated election information is accurate and reliable is still developing.

Closing read

Conclusion

The evidence indicates that AI chatbots have become a meaningful source of voter information for the 2026 midterms, with approximately one in five voters consulting them for election guidance. However, systematic testing reveals that these tools frequently produce inaccurate information, including invented races and misidentified candidates. The errors appear to stem in part from AI-built information sites that chatbots draw upon, suggesting a compounding effect where AI-generated content is used to train or inform other AI systems.

What is established as fact is that voters are increasingly using AI chatbots for election information and that these chatbots make significant errors when generating voter guides. What remains uncertain is the extent to which these errors actually affect voter behavior and election outcomes. The development of monitoring tools like MIT's LLM Election Observatory suggests that researchers and institutions are beginning to take the problem seriously, but the infrastructure for ensuring accurate AI-generated election information is still emerging.
