US Senate Advances Federal Deepfake Disclosure Bill

The US Senate has advanced federal legislation requiring platforms to remove unauthorized AI-generated images, marking a major step in American deepfake regulation.

Why this matters now

American lawmakers are moving to regulate artificial intelligence-generated synthetic media, with the Senate advancing a bipartisan bill that would require online platforms to remove unauthorized deepfake images. This legislation represents a distinct approach from European regulations, focusing on individual rights to likeness and platform accountability rather than comprehensive AI governance frameworks. The evidence comes from congressional records, policy trackers, and legislative reporting covering bills introduced in the 118th Congress and reported in early 2026. FIELD NOTE

The Senate's Deepfake Disclosure Legislation

The Senate Judiciary Committee advanced legislation requiring online platforms to remove unauthorized deepfake images generated by artificial intelligence. Under the proposed rules, platforms would need to take down synthetic media that was not licensed by the person portrayed. This approach places responsibility on platforms to police content and provides individuals with a mechanism to protect their likeness.

Separately, the Senate passed bipartisan legislation allowing individuals to sue over nonconsensual intimate depictions generated by AI. This bill addresses a specific harm caused by deepfake technology while establishing legal recourse for victims. The two legislative efforts represent complementary approaches: one focused on platform obligations and the other on individual legal rights.

  • Senate Judiciary Committee advanced bill requiring platform removal of unauthorized deepfakes
  • Platforms would need to remove AI images not licensed by the person portrayed
  • Senate passed separate bill allowing lawsuits over nonconsensual AI intimate images
  • Both efforts are bipartisan and address different aspects of deepfake harm

Federal Legislative Landscape

Multiple federal bills addressing deepfakes are currently pending in Congress as of 2026. These include the DEFIANCE Act, the NO FAKES Act, the Deepfake Accountability Act, and others, each with different sponsors and legislative status. The variety of bills indicates competing policy approaches and ongoing debate about the appropriate regulatory framework.

One bill, H.R. 5586 from the 118th Congress, specifically aims to protect national security against threats posed by deepfake technology while providing legal recourse to victims of harmful deepfakes. This bill reflects concerns that extend beyond individual likeness rights to include broader national security implications of synthetic media.

State-Level Deepfake Regulation

The existence of state laws adds complexity to the federal legislative process, as lawmakers must consider how federal standards would interact with existing state regulations. This dynamic has influenced the design of federal bills, which often aim to establish baseline standards while allowing states to maintain stricter rules.

Closing read

Conclusion

The US Senate's advancement of deepfake legislation marks a significant shift in American regulatory policy toward synthetic media. The evidence shows a multi-pronged approach: platform accountability for unauthorized content, individual legal rights to sue for harms, and national security considerations. Unlike European approaches that emphasize comprehensive AI governance, the American framework appears focused on specific harms and practical remedies.

However, the legislative process remains incomplete. Multiple competing bills are pending, and the interaction between federal and state laws remains uncertain. The final shape of US deepfake regulation will depend on how these different legislative efforts are reconciled. Readers should distinguish between bills that have passed the Senate, those advanced by committees, and those still in early stages of consideration.
