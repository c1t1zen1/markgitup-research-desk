AI Lobbyists Now Draft and File Regulatory Comments

As the FTC opens comment on AI accuracy, new filings show lobbyists using large language models to draft formal regulatory submissions, raising questions about the authenticity of public input.

October 17, 2026 · 9:02 PM PDT · 4 min read · 12 sources · Original family: AI Lobbying & Policy in Washington

Editor’s brief

Why this matters now

The Federal Trade Commission has proposed a policy statement addressing concerns that AI companies may be manipulating their systems contrary to consumer expectations for objectivity and accuracy. The agency is seeking public comment on the proposal, with submissions due by July 31, 2026. This regulatory process has become a focal point for AI industry advocacy, with various organizations filing formal comments to shape the final policy. The emergence of large language models as tools for drafting these submissions represents a new development in how AI influence is exerted in Washington policy debates.

Assignment angle: Focuses on the emerging use of LLMs to generate formal regulatory filings and public comments, a new layer of AI-driven influence in Washington.

FIELD NOTE

The FTC's Proposed Policy Statement

The Federal Trade Commission is proposing a policy statement regarding the application of the prohibition on deceptive acts or practices in section 5 of the Federal Trade Commission Act to companies that market artificial intelligence systems. The proposal specifically addresses concerns that AI companies may be manipulating the behavior of their AI systems contrary to reasonable consumer expectations for objectivity and accuracy. The FTC Act and other laws the Commission administers permit the collection of public comments to consider and use in this proceeding, as appropriate.

  • The policy statement targets deceptive practices in AI system marketing
  • Focuses on consumer expectations for objectivity and accuracy
  • Based on section 5 of the Federal Trade Commission Act

Industry Advocacy and AI-Generated Filings

Various organizations have begun filing formal comments in response to the FTC's proposal. One notable submission from the American Freedom Legal Foundation urges the FTC to adopt the policy statement in full, arguing that users expect AI systems, specifically large language models, to respond to their prompts without political bias. The filing claims that many LLMs, such as ChatGPT and Claude, have been programmed to prioritize ideologically liberal and progressive values as though they are objective, neutral positions. The use of sophisticated AI tools to draft and refine these regulatory submissions represents an emerging trend in how advocacy organizations engage with the rulemaking process.
