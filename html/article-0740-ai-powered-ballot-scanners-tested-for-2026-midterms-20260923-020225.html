AI-Powered Ballot Scanners Tested for 2026 Midterms

AI tools are being deployed in 2026 midterm election infrastructure, from ballot scanner audits to voter information chatbots, raising new questions about privacy, accuracy, and the future of the secret ballot.

Editor’s brief

Why this matters now

The 2026 midterm elections are seeing expanded use of AI in election infrastructure, moving beyond misinformation concerns to direct operational roles. Evidence gathered from multiple sources reveals AI being used to audit ballot scanner records, assist voters with election information, and potentially exploit privacy vulnerabilities in voting systems. The deployment ranges from sophisticated AI coding tools analyzing public election records to consumer-facing chatbots providing voter guidance, creating both opportunities and risks for election integrity.

Assignment angle: Focuses on a specific new deployment of AI in election infrastructure rather than misinformation or deepfakes.

FIELD NOTE

AI Ballot Scanner Audits in Georgia

A significant development in election infrastructure involves the use of AI tools to analyze ballot scanner records in Georgia. According to multiple sources, an individual used an AI assistant to process early voting lists and cast-vote record files obtained through public records requests. The AI was able to create "buckets of ballots and potential voters," enabling the matching of voters to their ballots in many instances. When ambiguity remained, the individual supplemented the AI analysis with public audit logs from ballot scanners and precinct check-in records, successfully matching most ballots to specific voters.

This deployment demonstrates how AI coding tools are "essentially supercharging laymen people to exploit vulnerabilities" in election systems, according to expert commentary. The ability to match ballots to voters using publicly available data and AI analysis raises fundamental questions about ballot secrecy, which Georgia's Constitution guarantees as a right and state law requires voting machines to protect.

  • AI assistant processed early voting lists and cast-vote records from Georgia counties
  • Created voter-ballot matching "buckets" using publicly available election data
  • Supplemented with scanner audit logs and precinct check-in records for ambiguous cases
  • Successfully matched most ballots to specific voters in tested counties

FIELD NOTE

Ballot Secrecy Vulnerabilities Exposed

The Georgia ballot scanner audit revealed a privacy weakness that had apparently gone unpatched for nearly four years. The vulnerability does not alter votes or undermine reported election results, but it does compromise the secrecy of the ballot—a foundational principle of U.S. elections. State law requires voting machines to "permit voting in absolute secrecy so that no person can see or know any other elector's votes," yet the combination of publicly available records and AI analysis tools made it possible to breach this protection.

The significance of ballot secrecy extends beyond privacy concerns. Without it, vote buying and selling become easier, and voters face increased risk of coercion from family members, political organizations, or employers. The Georgia case demonstrates how AI tools have lowered the technical barrier to exploiting such vulnerabilities, potentially making ballot secrecy challenges more widespread in future elections.

  • Known ballot privacy weakness in Georgia remained unpatched for approximately four years
  • Vulnerability compromises ballot secrecy but does not alter vote counts
  • Georgia Constitution and state law guarantee secret ballot protection
  • Lack of secrecy increases risks of vote buying and voter coercion

FIELD NOTE

AI Voter Information Tools

Beyond ballot scanner audits, AI chatbots are being increasingly used by voters to obtain election information ahead of the 2026 midterms. Research from the States United Democracy Center found that ChatGPT and Google AI provide incomplete and inconsistent responses to common voter questions, even as overall accuracy improves. The nonprofit conducted two rounds of testing in 2025 and 2026, collecting nearly one thousand responses from AI models submitted by users across six swing states: Arizona, Michigan, North Carolina, Nevada, Pennsylvania, and Wisconsin.

The research suggests that during ongoing elections with limited authoritative information available to AI models, these tools may not effectively push back against unsubstantiated rumors and misinformation. An ongoing audit at MIT tracks how popular chatbots—including ChatGPT, Gemini, Grok, and Claude—respond to questions about the 2026 midterms, providing continuous monitoring of AI performance in this domain.

  • States United tested AI responses across six swing states in 2025 and 2026
  • ChatGPT and Google AI show improving but still inconsistent accuracy
  • MIT researchers conduct ongoing audit of chatbot election information
  • AI tools may struggle with ongoing elections lacking authoritative data

FIELD NOTE

Language and Accessibility Considerations

AI voter information tools show significant performance differences across languages, with implications for election accessibility. Testing revealed an average 16% difference in response performance between English and Spanish for certain general questions analyzed. This gap is particularly relevant for Hispanic voters and other Spanish-speaking communities who rely on AI tools for election information.

The trend of voters consulting AI about eligibility requirements, electoral procedures, and security mechanisms is growing, especially given that such information varies significantly between states, counties, and election cycles. However, the language performance gap suggests that AI tools may not provide equitable service to all voter populations, potentially creating information disparities that could affect voter participation and confidence.

  • 16% average performance gap between English and Spanish AI responses
  • Voters use AI for eligibility, procedure, and security information
  • Election information varies significantly by jurisdiction
  • Language gaps may create information disparities among voter populations

Closing read

Conclusion

The evidence from the 2026 midterm election cycle reveals AI's expanding role in election infrastructure, with both demonstrated capabilities and documented limitations. The Georgia ballot scanner audit provides concrete evidence that AI tools can successfully match voters to ballots using publicly available records, exposing a real privacy vulnerability that had persisted for years. This is not a forecast or projection—it is a documented deployment with verifiable results.

The voter information chatbot research, while showing improvement in AI accuracy, indicates that these tools remain unreliable for critical election information, particularly during ongoing elections when authoritative data is limited. The language performance gaps further suggest that AI deployment in election contexts may create new forms of inequality in voter access to information.

What remains uncertain is whether election officials will proactively address the ballot secrecy vulnerabilities exposed by AI analysis, or whether the current reliance on public records requests and technical expertise to discover such issues will continue. The evidence suggests that AI tools have fundamentally changed the landscape of election privacy, making previously obscure vulnerabilities accessible to non-experts.

Five takeaways

  • AI tools successfully matched voters to ballots in Georgia using publicly available election records and scanner audit logs
  • A ballot privacy vulnerability in Georgia remained unpatched for nearly four years before being exploited with AI tools
  • AI voter information chatbots show improving but still inconsistent accuracy across six swing states
  • Spanish-language AI responses show a 16% average performance gap compared to English for election questions
  • The vulnerability exposed by AI analysis compromises ballot secrecy but does not alter vote counts or election results
