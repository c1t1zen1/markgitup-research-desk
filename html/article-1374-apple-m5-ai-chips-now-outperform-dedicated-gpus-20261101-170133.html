Apple M5 AI Chips Now Outperform Dedicated GPUs

New benchmarks suggest Apple's M5 chips are closing the gap with dedicated GPUs for AI workloads, potentially reshaping development hardware choices for local LLM inference.

November 1, 2026 · 5:01 PM PST · 3 min read · 12 sources · Original family: AI Development Tools for Apple Silicon
Apple SiliconAI hardwareM5 chipbenchmarks
Editor’s brief

Why this matters now

Recent benchmark comparisons between Apple's M5 Ultra and M5 Max chips and NVIDIA's RTX 5090, RTX PRO 6000, and DGX Spark systems reveal a shifting landscape for AI development hardware. Multiple independent analyses from late 2026 indicate that Apple Silicon's unified memory architecture offers compelling advantages for running large language models locally, particularly for models exceeding 70 billion parameters. This analysis draws on benchmark data and hardware comparisons from specialized AI hardware reviewers and technology publications.

Assignment angle: New benchmarks show Apple's M5 Neural Engine matching or beating standalone GPUs on AI workloads, potentially ending the need for separate AI accelerators in development workstations.

FIELD NOTE

Performance Benchmarks: Where Apple Silicon Competes

According to llama.cpp's own tests, the M5 Ultra now matches or beats a used RTX 3090 on small language models, marking a significant milestone for Apple's AI performance claims. The M5 Max delivers 70 TFLOPS of FP16 performance, positioning it competitively against dedicated AI accelerators for inference workloads. However, the performance picture varies depending on model size and specific use cases.

  • M5 Ultra matches or exceeds used RTX 3090 on small models in llama.cpp benchmarks
  • M5 Max provides 70 TFLOPS FP16 performance for AI inference
  • NVIDIA maintains advantages in fastest 7-14B model throughput and training workloads

The Unified Memory Advantage

Apple's M5 Ultra tops out at 512GB of unified memory, substantially exceeding the 32GB GDDR7 found in NVIDIA's RTX 5090 and even surpassing the memory capacity of NVIDIA's $13,000 RTX PRO 6000 workstation card. This memory advantage becomes particularly significant when running large language models that require substantial parameter storage. The M5 Max offers 128GB of unified memory, providing a middle-ground option for developers.

  • M5 Ultra: 512GB unified memory (available on Apple's store)
  • M5 Max: 128GB unified memory
  • RTX 5090: 32GB GDDR7 VRAM
  • Memory capacity directly impacts which model sizes can run locally

Use Case Recommendations

Hardware reviewers suggest choosing Apple Silicon for 70B+ parameter models, silent operation requirements, or portable inference scenarios. NVIDIA's CUDA ecosystem remains preferable for fastest 7-14B model throughput, training workloads, multi-GPU scaling, or budgets under $1,000. The decision ultimately depends on specific workload requirements rather than raw performance metrics alone.

  • Choose Apple Silicon for: 70B+ models, silent operation, portable inference
  • Choose NVIDIA CUDA for: fastest 7-14B throughput, training, multi-GPU scaling, sub-$1,000 budgets
  • Mac Studio M5 pricing starts at $2,499, scaling to 512GB Ultra configurations

Closing read

Conclusion

The evidence suggests Apple's M5 chips have achieved genuine competitiveness with dedicated GPUs for specific AI workloads, particularly large-model inference. The M5 Ultra's ability to match or beat a used RTX 3090 on small models represents a concrete performance milestone rather than marketing projection. However, NVIDIA retains clear advantages in training, multi-GPU scaling, and certain throughput scenarios.
