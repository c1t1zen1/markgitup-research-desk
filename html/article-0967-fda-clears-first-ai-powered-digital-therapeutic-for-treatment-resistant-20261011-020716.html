FDA Clears First AI-Powered Digital Therapeutic for Treatment-Resistant Depression

FDA clears first AI-powered digital therapeutic for treatment-resistant depression, marking a new era for prescription software in mental health care.

Editor’s brief

Why this matters now

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration has cleared Pear Rejoyn, the first prescription digital therapeutic (DTx) for major depressive disorder (MDD), developed by Otsuka and Click Therapeutics. This milestone represents a significant shift in mental health treatment, offering a non-pharmacological, app-based option for patients who have not responded to traditional antidepressants. The clearance reflects the FDA's growing role in evaluating AI-enabled medical devices and digital therapeutics, a process coordinated across multiple agency centers to keep pace with rapidly evolving technologies. This analysis draws on FDA announcements, industry reporting, and expert commentary on the implications of this approval for mental health care delivery.

Assignment angle: First-ever FDA clearance for an AI-driven, app-based treatment targeting patients who have not responded to traditional antidepressants.

FIELD NOTE

The Approval: What Was Cleared and Why It Matters

Pear Rejoyn has become the first prescription digital therapeutic to receive FDA approval for people with major depressive disorder. Unlike over-the-counter mental health apps, Rejoyn is a prescription-only treatment, meaning it must be prescribed by a healthcare provider and is subject to the same regulatory standards as pharmaceuticals. The approval specifically targets patients who have not responded adequately to traditional antidepressant medications, addressing a critical gap in treatment options for treatment-resistant depression. This distinction is important because it positions Rejoyn not as a replacement for existing therapies but as an additional tool for a specific patient population that has historically had limited options.

Regulatory Context: FDA's Evolving Role in Digital Health

The FDA's clearance of Pear Rejoyn reflects the agency's expanding mandate to evaluate digital health technologies alongside traditional drugs and medical devices. The FDA is responsible for protecting public health by ensuring the safety, efficacy, and security of human drugs, biological products, and medical devices, and this authority now extends to software as a medical device (SaMD) and AI-enabled devices. A dedicated office within the FDA coordinates the review of SaMD, AI-enabled devices, and digital therapeutics across FDA centers, publishes the FDA approved digital therapeutics list, and issues guidance documents to help the agency keep pace with rapidly evolving digital health technologies. This regulatory infrastructure was established specifically to address the challenges of evaluating software-based treatments that can be updated and improved over time, unlike traditional pharmaceuticals.

  • FDA coordinates review of SaMD, AI-enabled devices, and digital therapeutics across multiple centers
  • Maintains official list of FDA approved digital therapeutics
  • Issues guidance documents for digital health technologies
  • Regulatory framework established to keep pace with evolving technologies

Closing read

Conclusion

The FDA's clearance of Pear Rejoyn as the first prescription digital therapeutic for major depressive disorder represents a genuine milestone in mental health treatment. Unlike previous digital health tools, Rejoyn is a prescription-only treatment specifically approved for patients with treatment-resistant depression, a population that has historically had limited options beyond sequential antidepressant trials. The approval reflects the FDA's maturing regulatory framework for evaluating AI-enabled medical devices and digital therapeutics, with dedicated infrastructure to review these technologies across multiple agency centers. However, the clinical significance of this approval should be tempered by the reality that prescription digital therapeutics still face challenges related to payer coverage, reimbursement, and demonstrating robust clinical efficacy in randomized controlled trials. The modest RCT effect sizes typical of digital therapeutics mean that Rejoyn is unlikely to be a cure-all for treatment-resistant depression but rather one additional tool in a growing arsenal of innovative treatments. The true test of this approval will come in the coming months and years as payers decide whether to cover Rejoyn, healthcare providers decide whether to prescribe it, and patients decide whether to use it. The success or failure of Rejoyn in the real-world market will provide valuable insights into the viability of prescription digital therapeutics as a sustainable treatment modality for mental health conditions.
