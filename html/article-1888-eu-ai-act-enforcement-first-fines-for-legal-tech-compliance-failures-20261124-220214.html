FIELD NOTE

Enforcement Timeline and High-Risk Delays

A pivotal development in the enforcement of the EU AI Act is the phased activation of its provisions. According to recent regulatory updates, enforcement powers for General Purpose AI (GPAI) and Article 50 transparency obligations were switched on in August 2026. This date marks the beginning of tangible regulatory pressure, requiring immediate compliance for transparency and GPAI-specific rules. However, the Digital Omnibus legislation has pushed back the duties for high-risk systems, including many legal decision support tools, to December 2027. This delay provides a grace period for high-risk classifications but does not exempt vendors from the transparency requirements already in force. Legal tech companies must therefore manage a dual-track compliance strategy: adhering to current transparency rules while preparing for the high-risk obligations that will carry significant penalties.

  • GPAI and transparency enforcement powers activated in August 2026.
  • High-risk system duties delayed to December 2027 via the Digital Omnibus.
  • Legal tech vendors must comply with transparency rules immediately despite high-risk delays.

Extraterritorial Reach and Authorized Representatives

The EU AI Act's extraterritorial scope significantly impacts non-EU legal technology providers. The Act mandates that non-EU providers of high-risk systems designate an authorized representative established within the Union under Article 22. This representative is not merely a mailing address; they carry co-responsibility for compliance and act as the practical addressee for enforcement actions. This provision ensures that regulatory authorities have a clear point of contact and liability within the EU jurisdiction, effectively closing loopholes for foreign vendors. For legal tech firms operating globally, this means that compliance cannot be siloed outside the EU; it requires establishing a legal presence and accepting shared liability with a local representative.

  • Non-EU providers must designate an authorized representative in the EU under Article 22.
  • Authorized representatives hold co-responsibility for compliance.
  • Representatives are the primary target for enforcement actions against foreign vendors.

Closing read

Conclusion

The EU AI Act has moved decisively from a legislative framework to an active enforcement regime, with immediate implications for legal technology providers. The activation of GPAI and transparency enforcement in August 2026 serves as a clear signal that regulators are prepared to impose financial consequences for non-compliance. Although high-risk system duties are delayed until December 2027, this reprieve is temporary and requires proactive preparation. The extraterritorial nature of the Act, particularly the requirement for authorized representatives, ensures that both EU and non-EU vendors are subject to rigorous oversight and liability. Legal tech firms must integrate AI compliance into their core operational strategies to mitigate financial and reputational risks.
