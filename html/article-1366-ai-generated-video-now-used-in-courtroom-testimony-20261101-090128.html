Challenges in Assessing AI-Generated Video Evidence

Assessing the credibility of AI-generated video evidence is challenging due to the sophistication of modern generative AI models. These models can create highly realistic video content that is difficult to distinguish from real footage. Courts must consider factors such as the provenance of the video, the methods used to create it, and any potential biases or errors in the AI model. The emergence of deepfake technology has further complicated this process, as deepfakes can be used to create convincing but false video evidence. Legal experts are calling for greater transparency and standardization in the use of AI-generated video in legal proceedings.

Federal Rule of Evidence 707: A New Standard

Federal Rule of Evidence 707, which took effect on May 1, 2026, is the first national standard purpose-built for AI-generated evidence in court. This rule provides a framework for evaluating the admissibility and reliability of AI-generated evidence, including video. It requires parties to disclose the use of AI in creating evidence and to provide information about the methods and models used. The rule also allows for challenges to the credibility of AI-generated evidence based on factors such as provenance and potential biases. Legal experts consider this rule to be a significant step forward in addressing the challenges posed by AI-generated evidence in legal proceedings.
