Apple's On-Device AI Faces New Privacy Lawsuit Over Data Collection

Apple's privacy-first AI narrative faces legal scrutiny as multiple lawsuits allege unauthorized data collection, including a proposed $250 million settlement over iPhone 16 AI advertising claims.

Editor’s brief

Why this matters now

Apple has long marketed its AI capabilities as privacy-preserving, emphasizing on-device processing and minimal data collection. However, a series of lawsuits filed in 2025 and progressing through courts in 2026 challenge these claims, alleging that Apple collected user data without proper consent. The legal challenges span multiple issues, from Siri's recording practices to the advertising of AI features on the iPhone 16, and even extend to iCloud's handling of sensitive user content. This article examines the key lawsuits, their implications for Apple's privacy narrative, and what they mean for consumers.

FIELD NOTE

The Siri Privacy Settlement

One of the most significant legal challenges to Apple's privacy claims is the Siri privacy settlement. According to lawfold.com, Apple faces a $95 million settlement that would pay up to $20 per device to people whose Siri-enabled devices recorded them without consent. This settlement addresses concerns that Siri, a core AI feature, was collecting audio data beyond what users expected or authorized. The settlement highlights a gap between Apple's marketing of Siri as a private, on-device assistant and the reality of its data collection practices.

  • $95 million settlement proposed for Siri privacy violations
  • Up to $20 per affected device
  • Addresses unauthorized audio recording by Siri

iPhone 16 AI Advertising Claims

Another major lawsuit focuses on how Apple advertised the AI features of the iPhone 16. According to classactionbuddy.com, a proposed $250 million settlement alleges that Apple's advertising of the iPhone 16's AI features was misleading. The lawsuit suggests that the AI capabilities were not as advanced or privacy-preserving as marketed. This case is particularly significant because it directly challenges Apple's narrative that its AI features are both powerful and private, a key selling point for the iPhone 16.

  • $250 million proposed settlement over iPhone 16 AI advertising
  • Alleges misleading claims about AI capabilities and privacy
  • Settlement not yet approved by courts

iCloud and CSAM Storage

A separate lawsuit filed by West Virginia's Attorney General on February 19, 2026, alleges that Apple failed to adequately prevent the storage and distribution of child sexual abuse material (CSAM) through its iCloud services and iOS devices. According to tortsandactions.com, this lawsuit raises questions about Apple's data management practices and the security of user content stored in the cloud. While not directly related to AI, this case adds to the growing body of legal challenges to Apple's privacy and data protection claims.

  • Raises questions about iCloud security and data management

Closing read

Conclusion

The legal challenges to Apple's privacy-first AI narrative are significant and multifaceted. The Siri privacy settlement, the iPhone 16 AI advertising lawsuit, and the iCloud CSAM lawsuit each highlight different aspects of Apple's data collection and management practices. While Apple has marketed its AI features as private and secure, these lawsuits suggest that there may be gaps between the company's claims and the reality of its operations. The outcomes of these cases, particularly the proposed $250 million settlement over iPhone 16 AI advertising, could have major implications for Apple's marketing strategies and consumer trust. As more privacy lawsuits progress through the courts, Apple may need to reassess its approach to data collection and AI feature advertising to maintain its privacy-first brand image.
