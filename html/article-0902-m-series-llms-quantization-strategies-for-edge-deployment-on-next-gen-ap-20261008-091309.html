Why this matters now

This article investigates the technical necessity and practical application of model compression, specifically quantization, to run Large Language Models (LLMs) locally on Apple Silicon devices. The focus is on achieving high inference throughput while maintaining acceptable quality for real-world, edge deployments.

Evidence was gathered from technical guides and benchmark analyses covering various Apple Silicon generations (M1 through M6). Closing read

Conclusion

The evidence strongly indicates that achieving high-performance, low-power LLM inference on Apple Silicon is intrinsically linked to applying rigorous model compression techniques like quantization. Technical studies confirm the need to balance throughput gains from quantization against potential quality degradation.

While benchmarks provide concrete metrics on tokens-per-second across different M-series chips, the specific, prescriptive guidance on the optimal quantization strategy for a given LLM and chip combination is detailed within specialized research papers. Forecasts suggest that newer chips, like the M6, continue to offer advancements in local LLM capabilities.
