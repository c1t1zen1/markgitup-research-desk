FIELD NOTE

Activation of Enforcement Powers

On August 2, 2026, the European Commission, through its European AI Office, formally began exercising its powers to investigate and enforce the EU AI Act. This date marks the end of the voluntary compliance period and the beginning of mandatory adherence to the Act's provisions. National authorities across the EU are now empowered to act alongside the Commission, creating a multi-layered enforcement structure. The activation of these powers means that companies developing AI models face immediate scrutiny regarding their compliance with safety and transparency requirements.

The enforcement phase introduces significant regulatory pressure on big tech companies, extending beyond Europe's borders. Any company whose AI systems or outputs reach EU users is subject to these regulations. This extraterritorial reach ensures that global AI developers must align their practices with EU standards to maintain market access. The European Commission has gained new powers to investigate potential violations, impose fines, and order corrective changes at companies developing AI models.

  • Regulations apply to any company whose AI systems reach EU users.

Scrutiny of Major AI Developers

Major AI developers, including OpenAI and Anthropic, are among the first firms facing new scrutiny under the EU AI Act's enforcement powers. The European Commission has engaged with these companies following reports of AI models being involved in incidents such as hacking real companies. These engagements signal a proactive approach to enforcement, where the Commission is not waiting for formal complaints but is actively investigating high-risk models. The focus on these leading developers serves as a precedent for the broader industry, demonstrating that no entity is immune to regulatory action.

The scrutiny extends to transparency provisions, particularly Article 50, which requires clear disclosure of AI-generated content. Experts suggest that the first year of enforcement will likely focus on corrective measures and transparency compliance rather than immediate heavy penalties. However, the threat of fines remains a powerful lever to ensure adherence. The involvement of high-profile companies underscores the Act's intent to regulate the most impactful AI systems first.

Liability and Legal Implications

Parallel to the AI Act's enforcement, European Parliament lawmakers have proposed new liability rules to hold AI companies accountable for unintended harm. This proposed AI Liability Act aims to complement the AI Act by addressing civil liability issues, particularly for advanced models developed by firms like OpenAI and Anthropic. While distinct from the AI Act's administrative fines, these liability rules could significantly impact the legal landscape for AI developers. The combination of regulatory fines and civil liability creates a complex risk environment for companies deploying AI in sensitive sectors.

The legal sector is particularly affected by these developments, as AI tools increasingly influence legal decision-making.
