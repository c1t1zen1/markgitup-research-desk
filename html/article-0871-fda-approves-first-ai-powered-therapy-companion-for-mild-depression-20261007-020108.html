FDA Approves First AI-Powered Therapy Companion for Mild Depression

FDA approval of Flow Neuroscience's FL-100 marks the first regulatory green light for an at-home transcranial direct current stimulation device targeting depression.

October 7, 2026 · 2:01 AM PDT · 3 min read · 2 sources · Original family: AI in Mental Health & Therapy
mental healthAI therapyFDA approvaldigital health
Editor’s brief

Why this matters now

Flow Neuroscience's FL-100 has become the first transcranial direct current stimulation (tDCS) device approved by the FDA for at-home use in treating depression. The device requires a prescription and demonstrated a 57.5% remission rate in clinical trials. This approval represents a significant milestone in digital mental health treatment, potentially expanding access to therapeutic interventions beyond traditional clinical settings.

Assignment angle: Focuses on the first regulatory approval of an AI-driven therapeutic tool, marking a milestone in digital mental health treatment.

FIELD NOTE

Device Overview and Approval

The FL-100 is a transcranial direct current stimulation device designed for at-home use. Unlike previous tDCS devices that required clinical supervision, this approval allows patients to use the device in their own homes under a physician's prescription. The FDA's approval process evaluated the device's safety and efficacy for treating depression in an unsupervised setting.

  • First FDA-approved at-home tDCS device for depression
  • Requires a physician's prescription
  • Designed for unsupervised home use

Clinical Trial Results

In clinical trials, the FL-100 demonstrated a 57.5% remission rate among participants. This figure represents the proportion of patients who experienced significant improvement in their depression symptoms following treatment with the device. The trial results formed the basis for the FDA's approval decision.

Closing read

Conclusion

The FDA's approval of Flow Neuroscience's FL-100 represents a concrete advancement in accessible depression treatment. The device's demonstrated 57.5% remission rate in clinical trials provides empirical support for its effectiveness. However, this approval covers a specific use case—prescription-based, at-home tDCS for depression—and should not be generalized to other mental health conditions or treatment modalities.
