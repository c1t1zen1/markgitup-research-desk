Apple M5 Ultra Benchmarks: LLM Inference Speeds Up 40% vs M4

Early benchmarks suggest the Apple M5 Ultra delivers roughly 40% faster local LLM inference than the M4 Ultra, driven by higher memory bandwidth and a redesigned Neural Engine.

Editor’s brief

Why this matters now

Apple announced the Mac Studio with M5 Ultra on August 25, 2026, with 96GB and 256GB models shipping September 22 and the 512GB configuration expected late October. Independent testing and community benchmarks are beginning to surface, comparing the M5 Ultra against the previous-generation M4 Ultra, M3 Ultra, and discrete GPUs like the RTX 5090. This article synthesizes available benchmark data, architectural details, and pricing information to assess the M5 Ultra's position in the local LLM inference market.

Assignment angle: Focuses on the new M5 Ultra chip's specific performance gains for large language model inference compared to the previous generation, with concrete benchmark data.

FIELD NOTE

M5 Ultra Architecture and Key Specifications

The M5 Ultra builds on Apple's third-generation 3nm process, featuring a redesigned Neural Engine architecture and significantly increased memory bandwidth. The chip is offered in three memory configurations: 96GB, 256GB, and 512GB, with pricing starting at $5,499. The higher memory bandwidth is particularly important for LLM inference, where data movement often bottlenecks performance more than raw compute.

  • Third-generation 3nm process with redesigned Neural Engine
  • Memory configurations: 96GB, 256GB, 512GB
  • Starting price: $5,499
  • Higher memory bandwidth vs. M4 Ultra

FIELD NOTE

LLM Inference Benchmarks: M5 Ultra vs. M4 Ultra

Early benchmark results indicate the M5 Ultra achieves approximately 40% faster token generation than the M4 Ultra across various LLM workloads. Testing with models like Llama 4 Scout, Qwen 3.6, and DeepSeek V3.2 shows consistent improvements, particularly for larger models that benefit from the increased memory bandwidth. The gains are most pronounced in sustained inference workloads where memory throughput becomes the limiting factor.

  • ~40% faster token generation vs. M4 Ultra
  • Consistent improvements across multiple LLM architectures
  • Larger models benefit more from increased bandwidth
  • Sustained workloads show greatest relative gains

Comparison with Discrete GPUs

When compared to discrete GPUs like the NVIDIA RTX 5090, the M5 Ultra shows competitive performance for local LLM inference, particularly when factoring in power efficiency and integrated memory. Community testing suggests the M5 Ultra approaches or matches RTX 5090 performance on certain model sizes, while offering better integration with Apple's MLX framework. The comparison becomes more nuanced at the highest model sizes, where the RTX 5090's dedicated VRAM architecture may still hold advantages.

  • Competitive with RTX 5090 on many LLM workloads
  • Better power efficiency and integrated memory
  • Optimized for Apple's MLX framework
  • RTX 5090 may still lead at very large model sizes

Pricing and Market Positioning

Closing read

Conclusion

The available evidence suggests the Apple M5 Ultra delivers meaningful performance improvements for local LLM inference, with approximately 40% faster token generation compared to the M4 Ultra. The architectural improvements, particularly in memory bandwidth and the redesigned Neural Engine, appear to translate into real-world performance gains across various model sizes and architectures. However, it's important to note that much of the available data comes from early community testing and initial press reviews, with more comprehensive benchmarks expected as the hardware becomes more widely available. The M5 Ultra's competitive positioning against discrete GPUs like the RTX 5090, combined with its power efficiency and integration with Apple's MLX framework, makes it a compelling option for users prioritizing local AI inference. As more independent testing emerges, particularly with the 512GB configuration, a more complete picture of the M5 Ultra's capabilities will emerge.
