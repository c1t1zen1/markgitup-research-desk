Why video post-production could form a separate rights frontier

AI-assisted editing can be analyzed separately from model-training disputes or claims about whether generated material is copyrightable. Editing and post-production may involve questions about how existing footage, performances, voices, likenesses, music, effects, and newly altered sequences are controlled. However, the supplied sources do not document any particular tool, dispute, contract, court ruling, or studio framework involving those issues.

The distinction is therefore an analytical proposal rather than an established finding in the evidence. The Copyright Office’s reference to digital replicas and copyrightability supports monitoring these categories, but it does not prove that they have already produced a settled video-editing rights regime.

Conclusion
